# Informal Authority of Data Analysts

Analysts, often at lower organizational bands than their stakeholders, possess informal authority derived from their expertise in data and methods. This authority allows them to influence decisions, such as determining appropriate modeling techniques with senior leaders like a Chief Medical Officer. It involves resisting inappropriate stakeholder demands, such as requests for specific chart types, and asserting that certain methods are unsuitable due to data quality or methodological constraints.

Building this agency requires leadership development and confidence in technical expertise. Stakeholder management involves mapping internal team confidence and external stakeholder maturity regarding analytical requests. Strategies include identifying whether stakeholders demand inappropriate descriptive analytics or are ready for predictive and forecasting work. For resistant stakeholders, influence is exerted through strategic networking and identifying respected individuals within their networks who can help shift attitudes toward appropriate analytical practices.
