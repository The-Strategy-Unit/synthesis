# Leadership and data literacy in healthcare

The effectiveness of data-driven decision-making in healthcare is heavily dependent on leadership engagement and data literacy. Senior executives and non-executive directors often lack the necessary skills to interpret complex data, and these competencies are not consistently recruited for or tested. There is a critical lack of prioritization in developing analytical capabilities within the workforce, with current plans criticized for focusing on headcount rather than substantive training in data management and operational strategy.

Cultural disconnects further impede progress. Leaders often view analysis as a transactional service rather than a strategic partnership, failing to engage analysts during problem formulation. This prevents the integration of operational research skills into decision-making. Furthermore, there is a lack of psychological safety for analysts to challenge methodologically weak analysis that supports favored political propositions. To address these issues, boards must be held accountable for demonstrating understanding of detailed productivity data, and analysts must improve the narrative structure and clarity of their outputs to ensure they are accessible and actionable.
