# Network Data Lab and Discover dataset

The Network Data Lab (NDL) is a collaborative network of analytical teams commissioned and funded by the Health Foundation, operating across five locations: Northwest London, West Yorkshire ICB, Cheshire and Merseyside, Grampian, and Wales. These sites were selected for their advanced local ecosystems of linked health and care data. The Northwest London lab, comprising Imperial College Health Partners, Imperial College London, and the Northwest London Integrated Care System, focuses on deepening the understanding of factors affecting health.

During the 2024–25 period, the NDL focused its research on the elective care waiting list, engaging with NHS England teams, patient panels, and local elective care teams to identify research gaps. The programme is scheduled to conclude at the end of 2026, with outputs including briefings, policy blogs, and detailed analyses of NHS releases on elective waiting time inequalities. The analysis covered all specialties, examining waiting times, reasons for removal from lists, and healthcare usage before, during, and after waiting periods. Data sources varied by location; English labs utilised Secondary Uses Service (SUS) extracts and the Waiting List Minimum Data Set (WLMDS), while the Grampian lab used Scottish morbidity records. Primary care data was available in the English labs but not in Grampian.

The Northwest London lab utilises the Discover dataset, a linked healthcare database covering approximately 2.9 million current patients and 2 million previously registered patients in Northwest London. This dataset integrates data from primary care (observations, prescriptions, appointments), secondary care (A&E, inpatient, and outpatient services via SUS data), community mental health, social care, and waiting lists. Access is governed by a data access committee that evaluates projects based on public benefit. For research purposes, a de-identified version is used, retaining patient keys for linking journeys while excluding identifiable data. The technical stack for analysis involves SQL for data extraction from Snowflake databases and R for statistical analysis and visualisation, with code made available on GitHub.

A defining feature of the project is its strong Patient and Public Involvement and Engagement (PPIE), where patient and public partners assist in scoping research questions, reviewing statistical analysis plans, and providing feedback on interim results.
