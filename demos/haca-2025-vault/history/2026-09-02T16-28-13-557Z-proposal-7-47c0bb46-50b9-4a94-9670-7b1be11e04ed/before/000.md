# Demand and capacity modeling for acute care

A simulation model was developed to predict demand for acute care in the UK health and care system, with a focus on the 15-year mark around the year 2041. This model is part of the new hospital program and involves extensive analytical work, including the creation of reports and dashboards to help users understand the data and interpret the results. The complexity of the data and results necessitated significant effort to assist users in setting parameters and understanding the inputs and outputs of the model.
<!-- synthesis-claim:24100735e7b6329ee0c8d8ec37fc223b3f94d00d7586674455fdb73bfa4bf9b1 -->

However, initial user feedback was ad hoc, and the prioritization of developments was not formally structured. This lack of structure led to issues with rollout and missed opportunities, illustrating the consequences of not fully integrating product management principles and user-centric design in complex analytical projects. The case underscores the importance of engaging with users early to define the purpose and utility of the analytical solution, rather than focusing solely on the technical delivery of the model.
