# The Woodyard Model for Business Intelligence

The Woodyard Model is a business intelligence operating framework designed to reduce waste and improve workflow by treating data processing as a structured manufacturing process rather than a series of isolated tasks. Introduced by Dr Mark Far, the model employs the metaphor of a sawmill: raw data ('logs') are processed in a specific, sequential order rather than being hacked at simultaneously by multiple teams. This approach aims to establish a shared pathway for data work, ensuring that efforts are coordinated and reusable.
<!-- synthesis-claim:4253f4ae7cd5f5f018429aa3c0eebb5639339c9d3ac0157d799fafcab9691968 -->

The process involves distinct stages: data development (understanding raw inputs), data engineering (milling data into clean, repeatable structures or 'planks'), modeling and definitions (agreeing on standard definitions for components), data visualization (designing usable outputs or 'furniture'), and analytics and planning (determining how insights are applied). A key feature of the model is its use as a formal mechanism for accepting or rejecting work requests, thereby preventing fragmented efforts and ensuring that logic resides in a single underlying model capable of feeding multiple products.
<!-- synthesis-claim:4253f4ae7cd5f5f018429aa3c0eebb5639339c9d3ac0157d799fafcab9691968 -->

Implementation of the Woodyard Model emphasizes explicit roles, upfront data mapping, and a focus on psychological safety and neurodiversity awareness within analyst teams. By grounding business intelligence operations in craft rather than just tools, the model seeks to balance speed with quality, surface risks early, and create a culture where team members can contribute their best thinking without fear.
