# Healthcare usage patterns during waiting periods

Contrary to expectations, healthcare usage rates were generally higher prior to referral than during the waiting period or after treatment. This trend was most noticeable for A&E attendances. However, elective admissions showed a different pattern, with higher costs and usage rates observed while patients were waiting, followed by a decrease after treatment. These unexpected usage patterns challenged the assumptions required for causal analysis, specifically the hypothesis that longer waits would lead to a steady rise in activity during the waiting period and a decline post-treatment.

The observed patterns suggest that the relationship between waiting times and healthcare usage is complex and may not follow a simple linear trajectory. The higher usage prior to referral may indicate that patients are seeking care for symptoms that eventually lead to elective referral, while the decrease after treatment reflects the resolution of the condition. These findings highlight the need for careful consideration of baseline usage patterns when evaluating the impact of waiting times.
