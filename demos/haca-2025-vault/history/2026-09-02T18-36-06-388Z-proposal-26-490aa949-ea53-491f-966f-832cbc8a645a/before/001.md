# Demographic disparities in NHS waiting times

Analysis of the Discover dataset revealed significant age-related disparities in NHS waiting times. Seventy-three per cent of patients aged 85 and over waited less than 18 weeks, compared to only 63% of those aged 18 to 24, indicating that younger people generally wait longer. However, no major differences were found in waiting time proportions when analysing deprivation quintiles or other demographic variables. The distribution of waiting times was heavily tailed, with many patients waiting multiple years, making the mean sensitive to outliers. Consequently, median waiting times were used for further analysis.
<!-- synthesis-claim:21e7e25b42c84432bcfadc9ca15b05ae71af5d9a1967f2699049b493953ab7c2 -->

Median wait times showed that men waited slightly longer than women, and white patients had shorter median wait times than other ethnicities. Older age groups consistently had much shorter median wait times compared to younger groups. These findings contrast with the lived experiences reported by patient advisory groups, who expressed concern that older patients and those from lower socioeconomic backgrounds were being left behind or forgotten. The most significant differences in waiting times were found to occur across medical specialties rather than demographic groups, with neurosurgery and neurology exhibiting the highest average waiting lengths, whereas elderly medicine and mental health showed the lowest.
