# Data quality gaps in waiting list analysis

Significant data quality issues hindered the analysis of waiting list outcomes. The analysis relies on national data from the RT DT and does not perform extensive data cleaning; issues such as patients being removed from waiting lists due to administrative errors or gaming, rather than actual treatment, are left to the national team to address. Local implementations require careful data wrangling to handle anomalies such as patients on lists for extremely long periods, strange birth dates, or incorrect admission dates.

Over 40% of pathways lacked recorded reasons for clock stops, making further analysis of removal reasons difficult. Additionally, linking waiting list data to secondary care records to track healthcare usage and specific procedures proved challenging. These gaps resulted in high levels of exclusions during analysis, which prevented many findings from reaching statistical significance and limited the ability to draw robust conclusions about the impact of waiting times.

The lack of detailed information on why patients are removed from waiting lists limits the understanding of the effectiveness of current management strategies. Similarly, the difficulty in linking data across settings hampers the ability to track the full patient journey and assess the true impact of waiting on health outcomes. These limitations underscore the importance of understanding data provenance and potential inaccuracies when interpreting waiting list metrics and performance indicators. Addressing these data quality issues is crucial for improving the reliability and usefulness of future analyses.
