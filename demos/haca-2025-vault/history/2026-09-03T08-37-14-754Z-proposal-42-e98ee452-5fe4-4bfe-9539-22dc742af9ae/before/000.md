# User-centric design in analytics

A persistent challenge in analytics is the creation of sophisticated dashboards and reports that go unused by their intended audience. This phenomenon highlights a disconnect between the creation of analytical tools and the actual needs of users. The issue often arises when the focus is placed on technology and data rather than on the end-user experience, including interface design and user expectations.
<!-- synthesis-claim:24100735e7b6329ee0c8d8ec37fc223b3f94d00d7586674455fdb73bfa4bf9b1 -->

Incorporating user-centric design principles can significantly improve the utility and adoption of analytical tools. This involves moving beyond simply building 'beautiful dashboards' to ensuring that tools meet the specific needs of users, such as analysts who need to interpret complex data. Engaging with users early in the process to define the 'why' and 'what' of the analytical solution is essential. Without this focus, even technically advanced outputs may fail to engage with user understanding or fulfill actual needs, rendering the technical speed and power of modern data tools ineffective.
