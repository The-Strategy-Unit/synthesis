# Hospital Demand Drivers: Demographic and Non-Demographic

Hospital activity projections consider three main categories of change to forecast future demand. The first is demographic change, such as population growth and ageing, which typically increases activity. The second is non-demographic change, a category described as poorly named but encompassing everything else, including new medical technologies, improved patient expectations, and the general increase in healthcare activity. The third category involves mitigating activities or 'left shift', which includes factors that might reduce hospital demand, such as reduced smoking rates or better care pathways for frail and elderly patients to prevent falls.

To address the limitation that models only reflect the quality of their inputs, the project incorporates expert elicitation. Experts, such as those from the strategy unit, provide their beliefs about future scenarios. These expert opinions are fed into the model to generate precise statistical projections based on those beliefs. This approach helps hospitals understand potential futures, particularly for localized issues like frail care pathways, which central civil servants may not fully understand. The model acts as a reliable statistical engine that translates expert beliefs into quantifiable outcomes.
