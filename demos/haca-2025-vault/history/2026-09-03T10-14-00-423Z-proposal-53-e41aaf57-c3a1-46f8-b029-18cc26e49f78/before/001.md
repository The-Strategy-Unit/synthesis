# Patient priorities and agency in palliative care

Choice is critical in palliative care because serious conditions such as cancer, end-stage heart failure, respiratory disease, and frailty significantly limit options and reduce independence. Asking patients what is important to them restores agency and ensures care aligns with their values. Without these conversations, providers cannot know what matters to the patient. Responses can be unexpected; for example, a patient may refuse hospital admission due to concerns about pet care. Identifying priorities allows for tailored support, even when clinical circumstances are complex.

However, initiating advanced care planning discussions is often hindered by patient hesitation and clinician inexperience. Patients may feel tentative about raising the topic, fearing that suggesting end-of-life planning implies they are in their final year of life. Conversely, clinicians frequently report lacking the experience or confidence to start these conversations. Additionally, a perceived lack of continuity of care, particularly in community settings, reduces opportunities for general practitioners and other providers to explore patient wishes effectively. Cultural barriers also exist; staff recruited from overseas may hold views that conflict with standard palliative care practices, viewing end-of-life conversations as inappropriate. Significant education and training are required to address these gaps.
