# Demand and capacity modeling for acute care

A simulation model was developed under the New Hospital Programme, managed by the Strategy Unit, to predict demand for acute care in the UK health and care system, with a focus on the period around 2041. The initiative aims to inform the construction of billion-pound hospitals by providing robust, evidence-based projections. A core ethos of the programme is that the modelling work is owned by the NHS for the NHS, rather than relying on external companies, to ensure public benefit and transparency. The project has received recognition for its approach, though specific award details are not elaborated.

The programme employs a probabilistic modelling framework rather than relying on point estimates, arguing that point estimates are inherently flawed in the face of future uncertainty. This approach ensures that the analysis remains in the public record and can be scrutinised. The modelling process is collaborative, emphasising 'modelling with' stakeholders rather than 'modelling to' them, which is considered essential for high-stakes decisions such as hospital construction.

The project involves extensive analytical work, including the creation of reports and dashboards to help users understand the data and interpret the results. The complexity of the data and results necessitated significant effort to assist users in setting parameters and understanding the inputs and outputs of the model. However, initial user feedback was ad hoc, and the prioritization of developments was not formally structured. This lack of structure led to issues with rollout and missed opportunities, illustrating the consequences of not fully integrating product management principles and user-centric design in complex analytical projects. The case underscores the importance of engaging with users early to define the purpose and utility of the analytical solution, rather than focusing solely on the technical delivery of the model.
