# Analyst professional development strategies

Professional development for analysts involves building confidence and expanding skills beyond technical analysis through a combination of formal and informal learning. The **70-20-10 rule** provides a framework for this growth: approximately 10% of learning comes from structured courses, 20% from social learning such as mentoring and colleague interaction, and 70% from natural interactions and opportunities embedded within the organization. This model emphasizes the importance of networking, connecting with peers, and having supportive line managers who facilitate experiential learning.

Key strategies include **technical upskilling**, such as transitioning to coding languages like R or Python, often influenced by prior educational backgrounds. **Certification** processes, such as the Government Statistical Group (GSG) badging, provide formal recognition of statistical knowledge through tests and interviews. **Soft skills development** is also critical, requiring analysts to overcome discomfort with facilitation, chairing meetings, and presenting work by starting small and gradually building confidence. Mentorship and supportive teams are cited as crucial for identifying opportunities and nudging analysts toward growth, while community engagement helps maintain relationships with users and stay informed about emerging needs.
