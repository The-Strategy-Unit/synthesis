# Professional Development and Career Navigation Strategies

Moley advises that to shift focus between sectors, such as the NHS, policy, and academia, one should demonstrate relevant skills and interests in the target area while still in the current role. For example, while working in the NHS, she engaged with policy commentary; while in policy, she engaged with technical teams and learned Python. To influence academia from the outside, she used blogging and social media. The goal is to 'get in the room' by proving capability and interest beforehand. This strategy of strategic visibility ensures an individual's name is associated with the field when invitations are extended, making them a natural candidate for inclusion.

A management philosophy she advocates posits that the primary role of a manager or mentor is to remove barriers to enable junior researchers and team members to be brilliant. This approach views employment as a two-way transaction where the job must serve the employee's development and growth, not just the organization's needs. Managers are encouraged to invest in individuals' career progression, even if it means the employee leaves after a period, viewing such departures as a success of development rather than a loss. This fosters a better ecosystem and inclusive working environment. Moley also notes the psychological impact of uncertainty on professional decisions, describing her own 'allergy to uncertainty' and the challenges of managing seismic shifts in location and disciplinary context.
