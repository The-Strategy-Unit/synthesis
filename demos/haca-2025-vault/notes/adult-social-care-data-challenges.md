# Adult social care data challenges

The adult social care sector faces increasing pressure to use data intelligently but is often described as being more poorly served by quantitative data and analysis compared to sectors like health or education. Practitioners report difficulties in knowing where to begin with data, balancing competing priorities emerging from data against daily practice demands, ensuring balanced development, and finding the time and resources to learn from data.

Current issues include fragmented, inconsistent, or inaccessible insights; limited actionable evidence on what works; difficulty linking social care data with geographical information; and insufficient capability to use data at pace for day-to-day decision-making. There is also a noted 'silence' in data regarding lived experience, which partners can reveal. Rising national expectations from bodies like the Care Quality Commission and Integrated Care Systems exacerbate these pressures.

These challenges result in limited insights into adult social care, reduced knowledge of what works best for whom and in what circumstances, and a diminished ability to develop data skills and learn within professional roles. Addressing these gaps requires integrated infrastructure that combines public data, geographical information, and local context to provide customized reports and interactive data sites.
