# Data Linking and Infrastructure Challenges

Integrating diverse data sources, such as Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) data into Scotland, can enhance policy efficiency and cost-effectiveness. For example, better data linking allows for more accurate modeling of funds like the pupil equity fund, which costs £140 million annually. Prior to linking DWP data, models used to target this funding broke down at local levels. Linking this data allows for better verification that money is directed correctly, providing the gravitas needed to evaluate whether the policy is working to its full potential. This approach shifts the conversation from modeling assumptions to evidence-based policy impact assessment.

However, public sector analysts often face difficulties accessing advanced tools beyond Excel and PowerBI. Senior leaders who are not data literate often drive data solutions, favoring 'shiny new things' like the latest software versions over foundational data infrastructure. Effective AI and statistical models require sorted, high-quality underlying data; without this, new tools may cause more headaches than value. Building trust through small, effective solutions is proposed as a way to encourage senior executives to consult analysts before investing millions in new technologies.
