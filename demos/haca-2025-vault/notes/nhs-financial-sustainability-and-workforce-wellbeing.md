# NHS Financial Sustainability and Workforce Wellbeing

The NHS currently consumes about 40% of the public sector budget, a figure projected to rise to 60% in 30 years if it increases by at least 0.6% year-on-year. This trajectory is considered financially unsustainable and unlikely to maintain public support, especially if the system is perceived as not delivering for communities. The financial pressure is exacerbated by the rising cost of emergency admissions driven by funding imbalances between hospital and community care.

Workforce wellbeing is a critical component of this sustainability challenge. Burnout is attributed to working in a system where staff feel disenfranchised, unable to do their best work, and pressured to prioritise task completion over care and connection. Short-term initiatives and funding cycles further exacerbate these issues. Excessive bureaucracy, such as forms, thresholds, referral criteria, and processes, can worsen system pressure and hinder patient care. Reducing these administrative burdens is seen as a way to improve efficiency and allow staff to focus on doing their best for patients, supporting the broader strategy of permissive leadership.
