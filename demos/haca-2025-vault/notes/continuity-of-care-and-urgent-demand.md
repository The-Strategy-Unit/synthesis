# Continuity of care and urgent demand

There is strong evidence that increased continuity of care reduces demand for urgent care services. However, recent healthcare organization trends have often prioritized access over continuity, potentially undermining this benefit. The shift toward large community health centers is noted as a trajectory that may further weaken continuity of care, creating a territory of potential unintended consequences. Proper evaluation is critical to understand these dynamics, as the current preference for access may be counterproductive to reducing overall system pressure.

Patients tend to revert to parts of the healthcare system they perceive as most available or technically capable. Evidence from the four-hour A&E target suggests that when waiting times are substantially reduced, A&E becomes more convenient, leading to increased utilization rather than a shift to primary care. This dynamic raises questions about whether improving primary care consultation times would alter this behavior, though data on this specific intervention is currently lacking. The tendency to use the most accessible service highlights a challenge in managing demand across different tiers of care.
