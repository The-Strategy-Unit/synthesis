# Case studies in NHS waiting list performance

Specific examples illustrate the application of waiting list metrics across different trusts. In orthopedics, Mid and South Essex NHS Foundation Trust had the biggest waiting list with 18,000 people, compared to a Q-size target of 5,000, making it 3.5 times bigger than it should be. The Hillingdon Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust was identified as the most off-target relative to its size. Among large trusts, Robert Jones and Agnes Hunt Orthopaedic Hospital NHS Foundation Trust was in the most strife regarding being off-target. Conversely, Norfolk and Norwich University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust was noted as the most improved, with its Q-size moving down by approximately 10% each year.

An analysis of a Sussex hospital (identified by codes DRY and R, and RA2) revealed that TNO was the biggest bottleneck in terms of size. Other services, such as cardiology, were performing relatively better. Neurology was noted as being quite far off in terms of size but was currently improving, whereas the TNO service was not improving. These cases highlight the variability in performance and the utility of metrics in identifying specific areas of pressure and improvement.
