# Social Care Transition Probabilities and Demographics

The multi-state model predicts 10-year transition probabilities based on five years of observed data. For individuals starting in 'care at home' (including low-level packages, extra care, and supported living), the 10-year outcomes are: 42% no longer receiving care, 22% remaining in care at home, 4% moving to residential care, and 32% deceased. The model defines four states: care at home, residential care (including nursing care), not currently an adult social care user (including no care, informal/paid care, self-funders, or NHS continuing health care), and deceased. Transitions are allowed in either direction between the first three states. The baseline population consisted of approximately 5,000 people not receiving a package, 1,500 receiving care at home, and 500 in residential care.

Demographic analysis reveals distinct patterns in these transitions. Transitions to more intensive care (from no care to care at home) are more likely for younger people, individuals from Black and Asian ethnic groups, and those in less deprived areas; males are less likely to make this transition. Conversely, transitions from no care directly to residential care are less likely for younger people and Black and Asian groups. Transitions to less intensive care (from care at home to no care) are more likely for older people, White ethnic groups, and those in more deprived areas, potentially reflecting short-term rehabilitation packages following hospital discharge. Similarly, transitions from residential care to low care are less likely for Black ethnic groups, potentially indicating that White ethnic groups are more likely to use short-term residential packages post-hospitalisation due to family support limitations or self-neglect.
