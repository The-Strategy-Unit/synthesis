# Queuing theory metrics for waiting lists

Queuing theory, a mathematical specialization, is applied to health data to understand the mechanics of waiting lists. This approach involves developing metrics for managers to understand waiting list dynamics, including capacity planning and bottleneck identification. The methodology was initially developed in collaboration with a local NHS trust at Manchester University and presented to the Midlands Analyst Network. The principles are adapted to be accessible to NHS managers, focusing on the general mechanics of queuing systems rather than just NHS-specific contexts.

Key metrics include the size category, which assesses whether a waiting list is too large relative to performance targets by comparing current size to the theoretical size required to meet a specific target, such as the 92% 18-week target. The shape category evaluates the distribution of waiting times, which should ideally follow an exponential distribution; deviations indicate issues. The 'percentile pressure' metric determines pressure based on shape and percentiles. Additionally, the load metric measures the ratio of the arrival rate to the service rate. A load value of less than one indicates the service rate exceeds the arrival rate, which is considered good, while a value greater than one indicates arrivals are outpacing service, signaling deterioration.
