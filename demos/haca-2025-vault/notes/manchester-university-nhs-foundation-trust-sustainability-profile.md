# Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust sustainability profile

Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust is one of the largest acute trusts in the UK, operating across Manchester and Trafford with 10 hospitals and two local care organizations. It employs approximately 30,000 staff and serves a population of 3.4 million people across Greater Manchester and beyond. The trust has been calculating its direct carbon footprint annually since at least 2019. As defined by NHS England, this footprint stands at 74,000 tons of carbon dioxide equivalent per financial year, an emission level comparable to the output of 2,700 average UK households.

The trust aligns its operations with the NHS's legal commitment to reaching net zero under the Health and Care Act 2022. This commitment involves reducing emissions as much as possible, with offsetting reserved only for emissions that are impossible to remove. The trust views a greener NHS as a better healthcare system, linking sustainability with efficient resource use, care delivery close to home, and the prevention of hospitalization. These goals align with the NHS's 10-year plan and the broader theme of future health and care systems. The trust encourages experts to integrate environmental measures into their analysis, recognizing that all healthcare activity has an associated carbon cost that must be spent wisely.
