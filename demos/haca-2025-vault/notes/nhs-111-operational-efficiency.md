# NHS 111 operational efficiency

Between August 2024 and May 2025, the implementation of optimized messaging strategies resulted in a 7% increase in overall digital uptake, translating to more than 20,000 callers using digital options instead of waiting on the phone. Operational data indicates that an average call duration is approximately 11 minutes. Shifting 20,000 callers from phone queues to digital pathways resulted in savings equivalent to roughly 308 call handler days (derived from 285 hours of work, adjusted for 7.5 to 8-hour workdays). This metric highlights the potential for digital diversion to reduce workload for call handlers, particularly for non-emergency cases.

Notably, callers directed to digital pathways did not exhibit significantly higher callback rates within 24 hours compared to those who waited on the phone, even when outcomes were unsatisfactory. The study utilized randomized control trials to isolate causal changes, as concurrent systemic changes made trend analysis alone unreliable. In complex systems like NHS 111, concurrent systemic changes can obscure the impact of specific interventions when relying solely on trend analysis. The presence of multiple variables, such as IVR system updates and other operational shifts, makes it difficult to attribute changes in uptake to a single cause. Consequently, randomized control trials with control groups are necessary to isolate causal effects and validate the impact of specific messaging or process changes.
