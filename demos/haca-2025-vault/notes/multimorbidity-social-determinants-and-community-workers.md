# Multimorbidity, Social Determinants, and Community Workers

Multimorbidity is a primary driver of future healthcare demand, with 68% of people over 65 expected to suffer from it. Multimorbidity is increasingly occurring at a younger age and is more pronounced in areas of high deprivation and among ethnic groups. 80% of multimorbidity starts with eight specific 'gateway conditions'. Evidence suggests that better management of these gateway conditions—through prevention, early diagnosis, or effective management—can slow the pace of multimorbidity. Four key modifiable risk factors underpin these conditions: smoking, alcohol consumption, physical exercise, and diet.

Approximately 80% of health is determined outside clinical settings, primarily by social determinants of health where people live. The NHS cannot address health challenges alone and requires a partnership approach. Fundamentals of good health include feeling connected to one's community, place, and purpose. There is a widening health inequity, evidenced by a 20-year gap in healthy life expectancy between the poorest and most affluent areas. To address this, trusted, culturally competent community health and well-being workers are allocated to the 20% most deprived households to rebuild trust between the public sector and communities. These workers act as a bridge to healthcare services, assisting with social isolation and housing issues, and facilitating early diagnosis and intervention.
