# Overall Findings on Planned Care and ED Durations

Across 105 results from testing 21 mediators against five strike exposures, sizable reductions in planned care were observed on many strike days. These included lower rates of elective admissions, outpatient appointments, outpatient procedures, and non-emergency imaging. However, the analysis found no evidence that these reductions in planned care activities led to changes in ED durations. While emergency bed occupancy explained some duration changes across various strikes, the reduction in other planned care activities did not mediate the effect on ED wait times.

This contradicts the expectation that clearing elective work would improve ED flow. The speaker attributes this to the clear separation between emergency and elective patient flows, suggesting little contamination between the two. While hospital resources outside the ED were freed up during resident doctor and consultant strikes, this did not translate into reduced ED wait times via the measured mediators related to elective care.
