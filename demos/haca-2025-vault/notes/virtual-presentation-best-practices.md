# Virtual Presentation Best Practices

Effective virtual presentations require clear structure, engaging delivery, and technical preparedness. Presenters should begin with a clear introduction defining the problem or question, follow a logical narrative, and conclude with a strong summary. For shorter formats like lightning talks, pacing is critical due to strict start and end times. Presenters are encouraged to rehearse to avoid rushing and to keep an eye on the clock.

Slide design should prioritise simplicity and readability, with one idea per slide. Visual aids such as graphs and charts must be legible on various devices. Consistency in design and the inclusion of organisational branding help identify the source in recordings. Adding a conference hashtag to slides can facilitate discussion on social media.
<!-- synthesis-claim:c694f5302fdd7d6358cd12f1dcd947fd2f6dea83d2185f30fbbb2653d4850f90 -->

Engaging a virtual audience involves actively encouraging participation through raised hands or chat functions. Session chairs typically facilitate Q&A segments, but presenters can communicate preferences via panel chat. To ensure inclusivity, presenters should repeat questions read from the chat before answering them. Admitting uncertainty is encouraged as a valid response, with the option to follow up with individuals later.
<!-- synthesis-claim:c694f5302fdd7d6358cd12f1dcd947fd2f6dea83d2185f30fbbb2653d4850f90 -->

Technical preparation includes testing access to the platform at least one day in advance and joining sessions 10 minutes early to verify microphone, camera, and screen-sharing capabilities. Presenters should close unused applications to prevent performance delays and have a backup plan for presentation materials, such as saving slides on the desktop or emailing them to oneself.
