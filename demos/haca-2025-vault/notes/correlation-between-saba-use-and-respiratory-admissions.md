# Correlation between SABA use and respiratory admissions

Data from Dorset shows a reasonably strong correlation (R-squared value of approximately 0.5) between high SABA use and higher respiratory admissions, as well as between lower inhaled steroid use and higher admissions. While cross-sectional data shows association, longitudinal analysis suggests a causal link: PCNs that successfully reduced SABA use (e.g., by 12%) did not experience the rise in asthma admissions seen in other areas post-COVID. In contrast, areas that failed to control SABA overuse saw an increase in admissions. This implies that reducing SABA reliance is associated with stabilizing or reducing hospital admission rates. Estimates suggest that if all of Dorset achieved the same level of SABA reduction as the best-performing Primary Care Network, approximately 1,000 asthma admissions per year could be prevented, saving £1.5 million annually.
