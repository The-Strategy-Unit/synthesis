# NHS tariff imbalance in lower limb surgery

The NHS funding model for lower limb surgery relies on fixed national tariffs and block contracts, which create financial imbalances for complex cases. Under the national tariff, hospitals receive a fixed payment per procedure regardless of patient complexity, length of stay, or implant costs. Block contracts provide a set annual sum for expected activity. This structure incentivizes 'elective hubs' to focus on high-volume, low-complexity cases to maximize returns, while 'main sites' handle high-risk, complex cases.

A core problem is that tariffs do not match real local costs. Providers are paid a national average, but local staffing and implant costs are often higher. High-cost procedures like hip replacements frequently run at a loss, while low-cost procedures generate insufficient income to cover their share of fixed theater costs. Complexity is not fully reimbursed; tariffs assume a national standard patient, failing to account for the extra time, senior input, and longer stays required for complex patients. Consequently, performing more cases only improves finances if the cases themselves are profitable, which is currently not the case for many procedures at main sites.
