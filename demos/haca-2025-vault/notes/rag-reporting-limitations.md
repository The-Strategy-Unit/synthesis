# Rag Reporting Limitations

Rag reporting, or traffic light reporting, uses red, amber, and green colors to indicate performance status. This method has significant limitations: it is inaccessible to color-blind individuals, masks underlying data variation, and can lead to inappropriate reactions. Specifically, it causes 'flip-flopping' where managers panic when metrics turn red and relax when they turn green, even if the underlying process has not changed. This leads to 'sputtling'—overreacting to natural variation—and creates chaos rather than driving meaningful improvement. SPC charts reveal that what appears as a problem in rag reports may simply be random variation around a target.

Organisations often combine Red-Amber-Green (RAG) summary ratings with Statistical Process Control (SPC) charts in performance reports, despite these methods conveying contradictory messages. This combination is described as unhelpful and a 'retrograde step' that obscures the value of SPC. The practice appears to stem from a lack of understanding among senior stakeholders, such as non-executive directors, who may revert to familiar reporting formats like RAG ratings due to a lack of training or explanation regarding SPC interpretation. This trend was observed across multiple trusts during an IPR review, indicating a widespread issue rather than an isolated incident.
