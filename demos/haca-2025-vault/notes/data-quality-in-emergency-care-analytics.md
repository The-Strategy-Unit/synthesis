# Data quality in emergency care analytics

Ethnicity data in electronic patient records (EPR) presents challenges regarding completeness and consistency. While NHS England mandates and guidance have improved capture rates, significant variability exists. EPR systems may not code strictly by Office for National Statistics (ONS) categories, requiring work to match internal systems with reporting standards. Furthermore, ethnicity reporting can be subjective and variable, changing depending on the patient, the community group, and even day-to-day circumstances. This subjectivity is a recognized caveat in data analysis, though proceeding with available data is preferred over delaying work in pursuit of perfection.

Frailty data is identified as a significant area of concern regarding data quality. Despite confidence in other data quality checks, frailty information is noted as being frequently missing or incomplete. This gap is recognized as a key issue that trusts are actively looking to improve, highlighting a limitation in current datasets used for analyzing patient pathways and inequalities. A statistical method referred to as 'wakka blind regression' or decomposition, grounded in labor economics, is used to analyze sources of inequality. The technique takes coefficients from an initial regression exercise to determine how much variation is explained by specific data mix components, including acuity, frailty scores, and arrival hours.
