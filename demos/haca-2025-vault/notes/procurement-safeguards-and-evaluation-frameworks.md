# Procurement safeguards and evaluation frameworks

Healthcare leaders and procurement officers should avoid 'AI exceptionalism' by treating AI systems with the same scrutiny as other technology infrastructure. Key questions include whether the system works, is safe, has evidence of benefit, and offers economic value. It is crucial to ask why AI is the right solution for a specific problem rather than adopting it due to external pressure or hype. Procurement contracts must include mechanisms for continuous monitoring of performance and impact on the health system. If the AI fails to improve outcomes or exacerbates existing issues, there must be a clear mechanism to turn it off.

Resources exist to guide AI procurement and evaluation, including guides produced by regional health bodies and standardized evaluation frameworks under development in London. These frameworks aim not to evaluate specific products but to define the types of questions that should be asked and how to obtain evidence for them. This approach counters the tendency to adopt 'shiny' technologies without adequate justification or oversight, ensuring that ethical considerations and practical utility are prioritized over technological novelty.
