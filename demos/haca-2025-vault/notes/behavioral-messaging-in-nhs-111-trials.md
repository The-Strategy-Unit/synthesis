# Behavioral messaging in NHS 111 trials

A project aimed at increasing uptake of NHS 111 digital services used a three-phase approach: listen, design, and test. In the listen phase, surveys and focus groups involving over 12,000 callers were analyzed using a 65-million-parameter open-source small language model (250 MB) for topic modeling. This local model was chosen for safety regarding patient data and to reduce hallucinations compared to larger online models. Analysis revealed barriers such as callers believing their symptoms were unique or desiring faster outcomes.

In the design phase, the NHS behavioral insights team helped redesign IVR messaging using the EAST framework (Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely), focusing on 'Easy' and 'Social' levers. The test phase involved a London-wide randomized control trial comparing original messaging against 'Easy' (e.g., 'It takes 90 seconds for your outcomes') and 'Social' (e.g., 'You can reduce the strain on the NHS') messages. The 'Easy' messaging strategy consistently outperformed both the control and 'Social' messaging strategies. Over a six-week period, callers hearing the 'Easy' message showed a 7 to 12 percentage point increase in uptake for dental, emergency medication, and repeat prescription pathways compared to the control group. The 'Easy' strategy was also found to be the most effective for callers regarding respiratory issues, chickenpox, and sexual health. Consequently, the 'Easy' messaging strategy was adopted across all pathways.
