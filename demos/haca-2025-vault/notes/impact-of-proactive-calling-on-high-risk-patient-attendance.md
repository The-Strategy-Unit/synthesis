# Impact of proactive calling on high-risk patient attendance

Targeting high-risk patients for proactive telephone contact demonstrated a significant reduction in non-attendance rates. The high-risk cohort was characterized by a younger age distribution and higher deprivation scores. The expected non-attendance rate for this group was approximately 17%. However, for patients successfully contacted, the observed non-attendance rate dropped to 4.9%. In contrast, patients who could not be reached had a non-attendance rate of 40.5%, suggesting that the inability to contact patients is strongly correlated with non-attendance, potentially due to data quality issues regarding contact details rather than solely patient unwillingness. The overall observed non-attendance rate for the entire high-risk cohort was 10.8%, skewed by the uncontacted group.

The project was deemed cost-saving after accounting for staffing and employer insurance contributions, and it improved patient experience and reduced administrative downtime. Following the initial project, an endoscopy navigation program was implemented using two clinical endoscopy nurses to call patients, managing approximately 170 to 200 patients at one site. The program was expected to be evaluated in the new year, with anticipation of equal or greater success due to the involvement of clinical navigators.
