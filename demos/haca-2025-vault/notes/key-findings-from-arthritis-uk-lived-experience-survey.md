# Key findings from Arthritis UK Lived Experience Survey

The Arthritis UK Lived Experience Survey revealed significant negative impacts on the lives of those with arthritis. One in three respondents reported that arthritis severely or very severely impacted their life in the past year. Six in 10 people live with pain some or all of the time, and four in 10 felt their condition was not understood by others. Arthritis negatively affected personal relationships, sleep, hobbies, and family planning. Nearly half reported their ability to exercise was affected, and one in three felt their condition was not well managed. Qualitative accounts described constant pain, low mood, lack of energy, and feelings of isolation, with one respondent describing their experience as a 'living hell'.

Accessing effective treatment remains a significant obstacle. Nearly six in 10 respondents disagreed that they could access reviews to discuss their condition and treatment as often as needed. More than half stated that their current treatment does not help them live the life they want to lead. Additionally, almost four in 10 reported that difficulty traveling to treatment locations made access harder. These barriers contribute to the broader finding that one in three people feel their condition is not well managed, highlighting a gap between available care and patient needs.
