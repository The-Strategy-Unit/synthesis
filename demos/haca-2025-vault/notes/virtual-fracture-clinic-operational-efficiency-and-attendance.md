# Virtual Fracture Clinic Operational Efficiency and Attendance

The Virtual Fracture Clinic (VFC) demonstrated improvements in operational metrics, including breach compliance and waiting times. Breach compliance improved significantly, with breaches falling from 37.4% to 14.4%, representing a 61% relative reduction. The average wait for a clinical review reduced by 3.5 days to 2 days, identified as a strong indicator of improved safety and flow.
<!-- synthesis-claim:6b82bd315b24dc6ec27c4ac7f0b6c4df7fb555fc0bf5aff704f875e8958e90af -->

Implementation of the VFC resulted in significant reductions in face-to-face clinic attendances and Did Not Attend (DNA) rates. Total face-to-face attendances fell by 21%, with follow-ups decreasing by 29%, new patients by 4%, and walk-ins by 8%. DNA rates improved substantially post-implementation. For new patients, the DNA rate dropped from 23.5% to 8.3%. For follow-up appointments, the DNA rate decreased from 30% to 17.6%. These improvements were attributed to patients receiving clear communication and appropriate appointments.
<!-- synthesis-claim:6b82bd315b24dc6ec27c4ac7f0b6c4df7fb555fc0bf5aff704f875e8958e90af -->

However, on-the-day waiting times for face-to-face appointments increased in February 2025 compared to February 2024. Three reasons were proposed for this increase: increased complexity of patients seen in the face-to-face clinic, data discrepancies with missing waiting time data points, and a short post-intervention study period.
