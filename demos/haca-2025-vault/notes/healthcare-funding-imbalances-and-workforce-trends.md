# Healthcare Funding Imbalances and Workforce Trends

Over the last decade, there has been a significant divergence in funding flows within the healthcare system, contrary to policy ambitions for coordinated, personalised, integrated care. Hospital funding grew by approximately 21%, while community funding decreased by 4%. This financial shift is linked to a disinvestment in primary and community services, as well as social and community infrastructure critical for prevention. The imbalance contributes to an exponential rise in avoidable, mitigatable emergency admissions, which are the most cost-intensive part of the system.

The healthcare workforce has seen contrasting trends over the same period. While the specialist workforce, specifically hospital consultants, grew by approximately 168%, the generalist workforce required for an aging, multimorbid population has shrunk. There has been a 7% reduction in General Practitioners (GPs) and a 50% reduction in district nursing teams. This shrinkage in generalist capacity occurs alongside a rise in demand and complexity, creating a mismatch between workforce availability and population needs. Concurrently, workforce dissatisfaction is rising; the last NHS staff survey indicated that 41% of staff felt stressed, tired, or burnt out, attributed to working in a system where staff feel disenfranchised and pressured to prioritise task completion over care and connection.
