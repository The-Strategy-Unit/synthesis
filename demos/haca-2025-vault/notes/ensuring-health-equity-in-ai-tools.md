# Ensuring health equity in AI tools

To ensure health equity in AI tools, providers should be asked blunt questions regarding the makeup of training datasets and testing results, particularly across different locations. Tools should be tested with people from actual health and care systems to identify cultural insensitivity in how information is presented. Providers should be questioned about variable prioritization (e.g., variable A versus variable B) as this impacts equity. Additionally, 'red teaming'—a concept from cybersecurity adapted for equity—should be used to find holes in algorithms from an equity perspective, a practice that is currently underutilized.

A realistic worry exists that ethically challenging decisions, such as commissioning under stretched resources, will be diverted to AI. Policy documents often prioritize 'efficiency,' defined as doing more with less. This can manifest as outsourcing decisions to algorithms or training AI to make the most efficient decisions. However, efficiency does not guarantee effectiveness or equity. There is a risk that algorithms will be used to make commissioning recommendations without human oversight, prioritizing speed over equitable outcomes.
