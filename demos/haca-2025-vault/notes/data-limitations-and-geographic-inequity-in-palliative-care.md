# Data limitations and geographic inequity in palliative care

Nationwide research on palliative care in Scotland is limited by the absence of specific, routinely collected data on palliative care services. Researchers must rely on proxy measures, such as inferring palliative care needs from cause of death, hospitalisations in the final year of life, and prescriptions for anticipatory medicines. There is currently no nationwide palliative care data collection or minimum data set in Scotland that records patient preferences or advanced care planning, hindering the ability to assess whether patient wishes are being met on a national scale. A proposed minimum data set aims to standardise the collection and comparison of palliative care data, allowing for the analysis of how service provision and patient choices vary across different contexts. However, standardised collection presents challenges due to the onerous nature of data collection for services already operating at capacity.

Access to end-of-life care options, particularly the ability to die at home, is described as a 'postcode lottery.' Availability of services varies significantly by location, meaning patient choices are constrained by where they live rather than their preferences. In some areas, patients can remain at home, while in others, home care is not an option due to a lack of resources or service provision. This inequity complicates conversations about priorities, as clinicians must balance patient wishes with the practical reality of whether those needs can be met locally.
