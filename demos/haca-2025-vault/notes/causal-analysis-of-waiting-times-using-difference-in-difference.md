# Causal analysis of waiting times using difference-in-difference

A staggered difference-in-difference analysis was attempted to assess the causal impact of longer waiting times on healthcare usage. The methodology assumed a steady rise in activity during waiting periods for longer waiters, followed by a decline after treatment. However, the observed healthcare usage patterns did not meet these assumptions, rendering the causal analysis difficult to interpret. While some statistical significance was found regarding GP appointments, the direction of the effect was opposite to expectations, showing an increase in appointments following treatment.

This suggests that the initial hypothesis and methodology may need revision, potentially adopting a staged approach to assess methodology viability based on descriptive results first. The unexpected findings highlight the complexity of causal inference in healthcare settings and the importance of validating assumptions before proceeding with advanced analytical techniques. Future work may need to explore alternative methods or refine the existing approach to better capture the nuanced relationships between waiting times and healthcare usage.
