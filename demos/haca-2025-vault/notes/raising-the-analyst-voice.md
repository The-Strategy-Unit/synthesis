# Raising the Analyst Voice

Raising the analyst voice involves moving analysts from the background, where they are often hidden behind complicated dashboards or developing unused techniques, to a position of influence in decision-making. This shift is part of a broader career progression viewed as a journey from compliance to influence. Early in their careers, analysts may focus on pleasing stakeholders and delivering requests quickly to make a good impression. As they gain experience, they may become frustrated with unhelpful requests but lack the confidence to challenge them. Eventually, they may reach a stage where they actively seek to influence decision-makers, viewing it as their mission to improve data quality and decision-making through planned intervention and dialogue. This progression is linked to improved job enjoyment and greater respect.

To achieve this influence, analysts must provide objective challenges to programmes, even when uncomfortable, ensuring that a proper analyst voice is present on every board. The goal is to ensure that data expertise informs strategic decisions rather than being overshadowed by clinicians or CIOs. When challenging a senior stakeholder's request for poor data visualisation, analysts must plan their approach. This involves having a coherent story and solid reasoning, using examples to demonstrate the problems with the requested method. Timing is critical, and analysts may need to be persistent in securing time with senior individuals to present their case. Giving up at the first hurdle is discouraged; persistence is necessary to ensure that organisations make better decisions based on high-quality data.

Analysts are encouraged to exercise 'courage' rather than 'bravery' when challenging unhelpful data requests. Bravery is defined as spontaneous, bold action, whereas courage involves stepping back, planning, and engaging in gentle, reasoned debate. Being courageous does not require being loud or overly confident; it can be quiet and planned. This approach allows analysts to push back against requests that degrade data quality without appearing arrogant, by using humility and a coherent line of reasoning to demonstrate why a different approach is superior. This advocacy is supported by the Chief Data and Analytics Officer Network, which facilitates connections and professional development for senior analysts.
