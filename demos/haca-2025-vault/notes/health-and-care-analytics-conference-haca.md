# Health and Care Analytics Conference (HACA)

HACA is a national event for health and care analysts, described as being designed and delivered by analysts for analysts. Led by Chris Beeley, Head of Data Science at the Strategy Unit, the conference originated from recommendations in Professor Ben Goldacre’s 2022 report, 'Better, Broader, Safer', which advocated for the development of analysts, the adoption of reproducible analytical pipelines, and the open sharing of analysis within the NHS. The event is explicitly non-commercial, featuring no vendors or product sales, with the primary aim of fostering community, collaboration, and the sharing of analytical methods and findings. It aims to centralise the role of analysts within health and care systems.

The 2025 iteration was the third conference and moved to a fully online format, described by the organisers as a "learning journey" involving platform glitches and firewall issues. Despite these technical difficulties, the atmosphere was reported as positive, with high engagement from attendees and speakers who might not have participated in an in-person setting. The two-day virtual event recorded 1,150 registrations and included eight keynotes, 20 insight sessions, 38 lightning talks, seven workshops, panel discussions, interviews, and posters. Ben Goldacre closed day one, and Andi Orlowski closed the conference on day two.

The 2025 event was organized by the Strategy Unit, an NHS team, with support from volunteers. Due to a lack of NHS funding for this iteration, the conference relied on financial support from the Health Foundation, which provided the platform, and HDR UK to avoid cancellation. The conference faces uncertain funding conditions, with an emphasis on the need for continued funding and the valuation of the event to support the analytics community. Organizers emphasize the need for attendee feedback and advocacy to ensure the conference remains a fixed part of the analytical ecosystem.

The 2025 HACA was themed 'Rising to the challenge: analysis at the heart of health and care systems of the future'. The programme was structured around four sub-themes: shifting from hospital to community, improving system flow and reducing waste, targeting care investment for better impact and value, and advancing analysis and professional development. While these themes guided the content, talks were not strictly segregated by theme. Chris Beeley led the conference with support from Strategy Unit colleagues and volunteers, who coordinated the online programme, speaker materials, and attendee communications.
