# Limitations of Classic Synthetic Difference-in-Differences

Classic Synthetic Difference-in-Differences relies on minimising the sum of squared residuals, which is equivalent to finding maximum likelihood weights under the assumption of normally distributed outcomes. This Gaussian assumption is problematic when outcomes are on vastly different scales or when dealing with small counts and rates. For instance, comparing areas with high homeless populations to those with few homeless people causes the weighting scheme to fail, as the presence of zeros and non-normal distributions violate the underlying statistical assumptions.

Furthermore, standard Difference-in-Differences methods rely on the parallel trends assumption, which posits that treated and control units would have followed the same trend in the absence of intervention. This assumption is often implausible, especially when control areas differ significantly in scale or overall trends from treated areas. In staggered adoption scenarios, where units receive treatment at different times, standard DiD can induce bias, including potential sign reversals. These limitations necessitate more robust methods like SDID, which can selectively weight appropriate controls and handle heterogeneous treatment effects.
