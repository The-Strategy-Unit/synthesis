# Networked Data Lab and Discover dataset

The Networked Data Lab (NDL) is a collaborative network of analytical teams commissioned and funded by the Health Foundation, operating across five locations: North West London, West Yorkshire ICB, Cheshire and Merseyside, Grampian, and Wales. These sites were selected for their advanced local ecosystems of linked health and care data. The North West London lab, comprising Imperial College Health Partners, Imperial College London, and the North West London Integrated Care System, focuses on deepening the understanding of factors affecting health.

The NDL operates as a collaboration between five local teams and a central team at the Health Foundation. Its model involves selecting significant, complex topics that have not been previously addressed, often requiring data linkage and refined question definition. The network works closely with local teams for approximately one year on each topic, standardising analytical approaches (such as defining terms like 'intermediate care') and harnessing expertise across the network to spread capabilities. A key strength identified in its evaluation is the breaking down of silos by bringing together analytical, operational, and strategic decision makers locally. This approach exemplifies how collaborative networks can address long-term strategic analytical questions that are often too difficult for single teams to resolve.

The success of this national analytical network model depends on several critical factors. First, there must be genuine ownership and buy-in from constituents; the network avoids becoming an 'ivory tower' where a central team acts on behalf of others, instead leveraging local advances and supporting specific teams to perform work that benefits the wider network. Second, the network ensures members have the necessary skills, understanding, and access to tools to share work confidently. Third, strong collaboration between analytical, operational, and strategic decision makers is essential. Finally, the network demonstrates value early by standardising analytical approaches and tackling complex topics.

During the 2024–25 period, the NDL focused its research on the elective care waiting list, engaging with NHS England teams, patient panels, and local elective care teams to identify research gaps. The programme is scheduled to conclude at the end of 2026, with outputs including briefings, policy blogs, and detailed analyses of NHS releases on elective waiting time inequalities. The analysis covered all specialties, examining waiting times, reasons for removal from lists, and healthcare usage before, during, and after waiting periods. Data sources varied by location; English labs utilised Secondary Uses Service (SUS) extracts and the Waiting List Minimum Data Set (WLMDS), while the Grampian lab used Scottish morbidity records. Primary care data was available in the English labs but not in Grampian.

The North West London lab utilises the Discover dataset, a linked healthcare database covering approximately 2.9 million current patients and 2 million previously registered patients in North West London. This dataset integrates data from primary care (observations, prescriptions, appointments), secondary care (A&E, inpatient, and outpatient services via SUS data), community mental health, social care, and waiting lists. Access is governed by a data access committee that evaluates projects based on public benefit. For research purposes, a de-identified version is used, retaining patient keys for linking journeys while excluding identifiable data. The technical stack for analysis involves SQL for data extraction from Snowflake databases and R for statistical analysis and visualisation, with code made available on GitHub.

A defining feature of the project is its strong Patient and Public Involvement and Engagement (PPIE), where patient and public partners assist in scoping research questions, reviewing statistical analysis plans, and providing feedback on interim results.
