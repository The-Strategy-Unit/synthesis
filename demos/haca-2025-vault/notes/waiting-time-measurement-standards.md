# Waiting time measurement standards

Standards for measuring elective waiting times differ between England and Scotland, which affects the comparability of statistics across regions. In England, the Referral to Treatment (RTT) standard is employed, measuring the time from the initial referral to the first treatment or any other reason for a clock stop. This metric captures a broad range of waiting experiences.
<!-- synthesis-claim:2e12eb8c60d1461433da4628a366dc2c15411e1ad78b63ae47cd9eab46f69cdb -->

In contrast, Scotland uses the Treatment Time Guarantee standard, which applies specifically to inpatient waits. It measures the time from the agreement for inpatient or daycase treatment up until the receipt of that treatment. Consequently, statistics from English labs and the Grampian lab cover different populations and time periods. These methodological differences must be considered when comparing waiting list performance or analysing cross-border data, as the Scottish metric excludes outpatient and community treatment waits that are included in the English RTT measure.
