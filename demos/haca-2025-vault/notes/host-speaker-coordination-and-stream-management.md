# Host-Speaker Coordination and Stream Management

Hosts and moderators play a central role in managing the technical and logistical aspects of virtual presentations, handling the 'heavy lifting' of technical execution so speakers can focus on content delivery. Hosts possess a dual view: they can see what attendees are seeing to ensure quality, while simultaneously managing the backstage environment. A key feature is the 'panel chat,' which exists exclusively between hosts and speakers, allowing for real-time communication to troubleshoot issues, such as slide problems, without disrupting the live audience.

The transition from the backstage preparation area to the live attendee stream is controlled by the host. When a host initiates the go-live sequence, the system provides a 10-second countdown, giving speakers a moment to prepare mentally before appearing to the audience. The host ensures that the correct media, including camera and screen share, are active before the feed goes live. In workshop spaces, hosts can moderate the Q&A section by accepting or rejecting questions and manage participant visibility. They can move individuals from 'spectator' to 'participant' status, allowing them to appear on screen, with a limit of 12 participants visible at one time, including screen shares. After a session, the host removes the speaker and their screen share, returning them to the backstage area.
