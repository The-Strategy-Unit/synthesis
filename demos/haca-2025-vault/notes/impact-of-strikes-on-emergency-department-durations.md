# Impact of Strikes on Emergency Department Durations

Analysis of 1.7 million attendance records from 20 Type 1 emergency departments (EDs) in England between August 2022 and July 2024 revealed that patient durations in EDs generally decreased during strikes, contrary to some expectations. After controlling for case mix, temporal factors, and provider effects, durations were reduced by 12.6% (approximately 38 minutes) during resident doctor strikes, 5.6% during consultant strikes, and 4.5% during paramedic strikes. The reduction during nurse strikes was 1.6%, which was not statistically significant at the 95% level.

However, when resident doctors and consultants struck simultaneously, adjusted durations increased by 7.1%. These findings align with separate research from the University of Lancaster, which also observed reduced ED durations on strike-affected days for admitted patients. The study utilized a 50% random cycle sample of attendances due to computational constraints, though testing on the full sample for select models yielded substantially similar results.
