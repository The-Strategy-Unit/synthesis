# Impact of theater cancellations on fixed costs

Theater cancellations significantly increase the cost per case because fixed theater costs are spread over fewer procedures. An example from the data shows a theater day with only two completed cases (a total hip replacement and an ACL reconstruction) and one cancelled case resulted in a net loss of £21,000. Even when two additional arthroscopies were added to the cancelled slot, the total net income increased by £9,900 against a new total cost of £9,000, yielding a net profit of £922 from the additions. However, this still left the overall day in a loss of approximately £20,000.

This demonstrates that simply adding low-cost procedures to fill gaps does not necessarily offset the substantial losses incurred from high-cost procedures or the underlying fixed costs. Theater non-pay costs, such as swabs and disposable equipment, are real but not a primary source for significant savings. These items are essential for patient safety with little room for substitution. Therefore, financial improvement should focus on staffing efficiency, implant control, and throughput rather than cutting essential consumables.
