# AI as a systems-level technology in healthcare

Artificial intelligence in healthcare is best understood as a systems-level technology rather than a simple tool replacement. Its development and deployment touch every point of the care service, from data collection and curation to model training and algorithm deployment. This systemic nature allows AI to 'redesign reality' by altering the fabric of health and care. Key affordances include separating symptoms from diagnosis, separating knowledge about the body from the body itself (diagnosing data rather than people), and separating clinical knowledge from the clinician.

Conversely, AI joins together the patient and their data, making electronic health records the 'single source of truth.' It also merges service analytics with clinical decision support and joins research with practice in real time. Finally, it connects big tech with healthcare, often introducing products from outside the health and care analytics community that lack foundational understanding of the system. This integration means AI is not merely an add-on but a transformative force that restructures clinical workflows and data relationships.
