# Reasons for leaving NHS waiting lists

The reasons for leaving NHS waiting lists vary significantly by specialty and location. In regions such as Trent and East Midlands, less than half of pathways ended due to the receipt of first treatment (48%), while nearly a third ended due to a 'decision not to treat'. This category is broad and may include patients who drop off to pursue private options, which are not separately identified in the data.

Specialties with long waiting times, such as neurology, gynaecology, and ENT, showed relatively high proportions of pathways ending due to decisions not to treat. This raises questions about whether prolonged waits alter clinical needs or prompt patients to seek alternative care. Conversely, plastic surgery had the highest rate of clock stops due to first treatment, while elderly medicine frequently ended pathways due to the initiation of active monitoring. 'Did not attend' rates were generally low (<1%) but were double for the most deprived quintile compared to the least deprived, highlighting potential access barriers for disadvantaged populations.
