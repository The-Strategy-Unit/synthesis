# Waiting time inequalities in emergency care

Statistical analysis of waiting times using OLS regressions over 22 months indicates that white patients wait longer than other ethnic groups, a relationship that is statistically significant. The unadjusted difference is large, but adding controls for age reduces the coefficient, though it remains significant. Significant differences are observed between Asian and white populations, as well as Black British and white populations. When decomposing the gap between white and Asian groups, the waiting time gap is larger in the admitted population (approximately 2 standard deviations) compared to the non-admitted population.

Analysis of waiting times by deprivation quintiles shows that unadjusted results are large, but adding controls for age causes the signs to flip. A slight deprivation gap emerges between the most and least deprived groups until arrival hour is added, after which most relationships fade to insignificance. In the admitted population, meaningful differences persist, and adding age causes a big change, though frailty, acuity, and arrival hour add little explanatory power. The deprivation gap in the admitted population is slightly larger than in the non-admitted population. However, unlike the ethnicity gap, a larger percentage of the deprivation gap in the non-admitted population remains unexplained by case mix, suggesting a bigger role for ED operational behavior in addressing deprivation-related disparities in this group.
