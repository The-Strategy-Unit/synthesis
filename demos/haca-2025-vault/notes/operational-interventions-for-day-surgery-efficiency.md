# Operational interventions for day surgery efficiency

Actionable changes to improve day surgery discharge rates were co-designed with clinicians and hospital managers based on data insights. One key intervention involved removing thyroid surgery patients from key performance indicators (KPIs). These patients had an extremely high likelihood of delayed discharge, with an odds ratio of 117, due to sound medical decisions that were not amenable to change. Excluding them prevented the artificial inflation of delay metrics and allowed for more accurate performance tracking.

Another intervention focused on modifying prescribing practices by reordering the hospital's drop-down menu for opioids. Drugs with the smallest impact on discharge delays, such as fentanyl, were moved to the top of the list. This change aimed to counteract the tendency of busy clinicians to select the first available option, thereby reducing the prescription of high-delay-risk opioids like dihydrocodeine. Additionally, efforts were made to evaluate and improve the consistency of opioid prescriptions between the surgery room and the recovery room. These strategies leverage known predictors to achieve the target of 95% same-day discharges, emphasizing the importance of aligning operational processes with clinical evidence.
