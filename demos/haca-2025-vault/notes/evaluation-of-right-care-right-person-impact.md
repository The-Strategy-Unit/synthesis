# Evaluation of Right Care Right Person impact

An evaluation conducted by the Suffolk Office of Data and Analytics assessed the impact of the Right Care Right Person initiative on partner agencies in Suffolk. The study aimed to determine if referrals from the police led to increased demand on other services. The evaluation collated demand data from Suffolk County Council and health systems, matching it against police referral data. It focused on core volumes and overall demand rather than tracking identifiable individuals, thus avoiding the need for Data Processing Agreements or Information Sharing Agreements.

Quantitative analysis revealed a clear correlation between increased police referrals and increased demand on ambulance services; as referrals for psychiatric conditions rose, the number of ambulances dispatched with a psychiatric flag also increased. However, no significant correlation was found for demand on 111 calls, referrals into children and adult social care, multi-agency safeguarding hub concerns, emergency care calls related to alcohol and substance abuse, or mental health referrals involving police. These findings alleviated initial concerns that partner services would be overwhelmed by increased demand, given existing resource pressures. The analysis relied on aggregated quantitative data, which may not capture localized effects within specific parts of the services.
