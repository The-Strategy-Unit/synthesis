# National Neighbourhood Health Implementation Programme

The National Neighbourhood Health Implementation Programme (NHIP) is a major national initiative led by NHS England to advance neighbourhood health. Led by Dr Minal Bakhai, the programme represents a shift from service-utilisation metrics to whole-person outcomes, operating on the premise that health is wider than healthcare and that 99.9% of health happens at home. The first wave involves 43 sites selected from 141 applications, representing 83% of the country. Rather than relying on traditional pilot-and-spread models, the programme aims to systematically spread existing knowledge across multiple places.

A core component of the NHIP is the adoption of a 'tight-loose-tight' implementation model. This governance framework is 'tight' on shared mission, accountability, and outcomes, while remaining 'loose' on implementation details to allow for local adaptation. This approach recognises that there is no one-size-fits-all solution and that interventions must be adapted to local contexts. The programme connects local teams with national policy teams to co-produce solutions, hardwire changes through funding flows and performance frameworks, and work across government departments such as the Cabinet Office and DCMS.

The programme emphasises a mindset shift from deficit-based to strength-based approaches, moving from 'what is the matter with you' to 'what matters to you'. It aims to embed neighbourhood health practices by learning from past failures in large-scale change initiatives, particularly regarding the necessity of trusted relationships and the integration of data sets to measure effective interventions.
