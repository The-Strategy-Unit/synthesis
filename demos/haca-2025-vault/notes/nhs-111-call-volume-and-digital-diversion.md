# NHS 111 call volume and digital diversion

NHS 111 receives approximately 7,000 calls daily for emergencies and urgent care across England, with about 25,000 of these originating from London areas. Up to 70% of these calls concern low acuity issues, such as toothaches, medication refills, or other non-life-threatening concerns. This volume increases the burden on NHS-hired call handlers and contributes to longer wait times for callers with genuine emergencies.
<!-- synthesis-claim:25c434c030749082931259480e71effbdabe36d3fe2688d6f4e1e7319b419dcb -->

To reduce telephone call volume, NHS 111 offers a self-service digital option. Callers are triaged using natural language processing and then hear an interactive voice response (IVR) message. The IVR asks if the caller prefers the digital option, which provides the same outcomes as speaking to a handler, versus waiting on the telephone. If the caller agrees, they are sent a link to the appropriate digital service, such as a dentist appointment for dental issues. Prior to optimization, the uptake rate for this option was approximately 17%.
