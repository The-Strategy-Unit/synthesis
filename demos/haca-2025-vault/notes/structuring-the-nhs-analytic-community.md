# Structuring the NHS Analytic Community

The NHS analytic community currently lacks sufficient structure to retain and scale individual expertise. Without formal systems such as training programmes, textbooks, online courses, and conferences, individual contributions and insights are frequently lost when analysts leave or complete tasks. A mature, structured community would be indicated by specific metrics, including the annual hosting of three conferences similar to 'Hacker', the publication of three textbooks on practical NHS data use within a single year, and the availability of twenty free online courses focused on practical data use rather than generic statistics.

Further indicators of a robust community include the appointment of ten professors of NHS data analytics in UK universities and ensuring that every NHS organisation with more than one hundred doctors employs more than five NHS data analysts. Currently, analytic roles are categorised under administrative or clerical bands in the Agenda for Change pay arrangements, rather than technical or scientific categories. This classification makes senior progression difficult without moving into management, industry, or focusing on esoteric methods such as artificial intelligence. Parity in pay with medical professionals is suggested as a necessary condition for retaining high-level analytical talent.
