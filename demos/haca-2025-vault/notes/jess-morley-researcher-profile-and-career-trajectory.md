# Jess Morley: Researcher Profile and Career Trajectory

Jess Morley is an associate research scientist at the Yale Digital Ethics Center. Her career path includes significant roles within the NHS, policy advisory positions, and academic research at the Bennett Institute (formerly the Data Lab) at the University of Oxford. At Oxford, she contributed to the OpenSAFELY project and the 'Better, Broader, Safer' report. Her work is characterized by a focus on bridging the gaps between academia, policy, and clinical practice in digital health.
<!-- synthesis-claim:e692ea9f8568d58f1e752fa1cc0991cd812688b48cec1d27d8ac8f75e3361b06 -->

Morley’s move to the United States was influenced by the relocation of her PhD supervisor, Luciano Floridi, and advice from external supervisor Professor Pearse Keane, who encouraged UK-based health researchers to gain US experience to test assumptions about healthcare systems. The transition involved establishing a new center from the ground up, presenting logistical challenges but offering greater academic freedom compared to her previous constrained roles. She aims to eventually return to the UK as an associate professor running her own lab.
<!-- synthesis-claim:e692ea9f8568d58f1e752fa1cc0991cd812688b48cec1d27d8ac8f75e3361b06 -->

Key professional milestones include gaining access to full electronic health records via OpenSAFELY during the COVID-19 pandemic, which she describes as a major collective achievement for the Bennett Institute. She also highlights the publication of the Goldacre review as a significant collaborative effort. Morley advises against rigid five-year career plans, viewing them as sources of distraction, and emphasizes the importance of demonstrating relevant skills and interests to navigate between sectors such as the NHS, policy, and academia.
