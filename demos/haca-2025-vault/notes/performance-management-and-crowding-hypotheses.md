# Performance Management and Crowding Hypotheses

During resident doctor and consultant strikes, staff appeared to make extra efforts to admit or discharge patients just before the 4-hour target deadline. This intensified management of performance against the 4-hour target led to reductions in average ED durations. Conversely, during paramedic strikes, the opposite effect was observed. This suggests that targeted administrative or clinical interventions aimed at meeting specific time targets can significantly influence ED throughput, independent of broader changes in hospital resource allocation or elective care cancellations.

The speaker also speculates that reduced crowding may be a key unmeasured factor contributing to improved ED flow during strikes. When ED departments are less crowded, the transit of patients through the unit may speed up. The hypothesis is that proactive management of patients upon arrival during strike days might have reduced crowding, thereby improving flow. However, this variable was not included in the causal models due to concerns about feedback loops and cyclic relationships in the causal diagrams, which could invalidate the models. This remains an open area for further investigation into the dynamics of ED congestion.
