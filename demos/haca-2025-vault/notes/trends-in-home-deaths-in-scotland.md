# Trends in home deaths in Scotland

Research using routinely collected data from the NHS and National Records of Scotland indicates a significant shift in end-of-life care locations following the COVID-19 pandemic. The number of home deaths in Scotland increased by approximately 35% during the first year of the pandemic. Since the pandemic, approximately one-third of all deaths in Scotland occur at home, an increase from about 25% prior to the pandemic. This shift represents a massive increase in engagement with community-based services.

Analysis of death registrations linked to health service use between 2015 and 2021 reveals changes in the demographic profile of individuals dying at home. Compared to pre-pandemic trends, people dying at home are now older, more likely to be male and married, and exhibit a higher level of multimorbidity. They are also prescribed a wider range of medicines. Hospital admissions for these individuals were shorter, with earlier discharge home, resulting in more time spent in the community during the final 12 months of life.
