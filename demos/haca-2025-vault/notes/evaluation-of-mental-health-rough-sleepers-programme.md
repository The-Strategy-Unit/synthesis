# Evaluation of Mental Health Rough Sleepers Programme

The evaluation of the Mental Health Rough Sleepers Programme utilised a combination of qualitative assessment, cost surveys, and cost-consequence analysis, alongside a quantitative impact evaluation. The quantitative component employed advanced causal inference techniques to address the complexities of staggered treatment adoption and heterogeneous impacts across different services and time points.
<!-- synthesis-claim:709e547f8fa6aef75ab7c7a19d39358d2b068a13055e48854f5f03b236c49d6e -->

The findings indicated no statistically significant impact on accessibility measures, although all metrics moved in the desired direction. Similarly, there was no detectable impact on the total number of emergency admissions, with wide confidence intervals attributed to methodological challenges in data identification. However, the evaluation did identify significant reductions in specific acute care metrics. There was a reduction of 13.61 A&E attendances per month per service by people who had been homeless within the study period (p < 0.01). Additionally, the length of stay for emergency admissions decreased by 0.28 days per admission, a finding supported by a very small p-value and derived using a more conventional synthetic difference-in-differences method.
