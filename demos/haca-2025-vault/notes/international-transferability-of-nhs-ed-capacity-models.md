# International transferability of NHS ED capacity models

The collaboration between NHS teams and Scandinavian counterparts demonstrated that emergency department (ED) capacity models developed in England can be adapted for international use. This transferability challenges the notion that operational solutions are strictly bound to their origin country's regulatory framework. However, successful transfer required specific technical and operational adjustments. These included modifying the software package to handle different data types and replacing the UK-specific four-hour ED admission target with alternative metrics for representing demand and processing times.

The adaptation process highlighted the importance of flexible modeling that can account for local regulatory and operational differences while maintaining the core logic of the original solution. Management support was critical to this success; in the Scandinavian context, the project was viewed as an opportunity to trial a proven solution, generating excitement among ED clinicians, ward managers, and technical staff. In the UK, sustained support from senior figures, such as the Director of Operations at UCLH's coordination center, has been vital for the continued use and loyalty to the application. This broad engagement suggests that demonstrating tangible benefits to diverse stakeholders facilitates the adoption of new operational technologies.
