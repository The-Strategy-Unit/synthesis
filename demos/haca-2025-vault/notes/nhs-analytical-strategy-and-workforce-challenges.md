# NHS Analytical Strategy and Workforce Challenges

The NHS analytical community faces significant challenges stemming from organizational reorganizations, resource shrinkage, and policy uncertainty. Specific pressures include 50% budget cuts, organizational mergers, and the closure of Clinical Services Units (CSUs), which impact systems unequally across the country. While the role of intelligence in driving strategic commissioning is elevated, there is no anticipated increase in capacity to match these expectations. The NHS possesses rich datasets but often fails to utilise them to their maximum potential due to a legacy lack of deep data science skills, particularly within Integrated Care Boards (ICBs) and former clinical commissioning groups. Unlike central government, there is often no expectation that advanced analytics capabilities are readily available.

Delivering analytically informed decisions with reduced resources requires increased national investment in analyst development and a new approach to work. This involves embracing analytical specialization, effective collaboration, and securing economies of scale by avoiding duplication of efforts. A proposed solution gaining momentum is the creation of a national improvement network for analyst teams, referred to as a 'decision support network'. This network aims to address the structural challenges facing the workforce and improve the efficiency and impact of analytical work across the health service.

The National Decision Support Network is a proposed collaborative model owned by ICBs, designed to strengthen analytical capacity and improve strategic decision-making across health and care. Developed through co-production involving the Strategy Unit, the Health Foundation, and ICB colleagues across seven regions, the proposal aims to build a coherent, sustainable analytical infrastructure. The network is structured around three key components: local intelligence functions, a collaborative network, and a national development centre. This structure seeks to align analytical teams with the new NHS operating model set out in the 10-year health plan, clarifying the value proposition of different teams and identifying functions to be performed at national, regional, and local levels.

First, local intelligence functions within ICBs serve as the analytical backbone; the network supports their development and provides access to specialist expertise that cannot be maintained locally. Second, a collaborative network brings these functions together to reduce duplication, unlock economies of scale, facilitate the open sharing of methods and models, and identify common questions to be answered once on behalf of all systems. Third, a national development centre, proposed to be led by the Strategy Unit in partnership with other specialist teams, would manage the network, ensure methodological rigour, deliver shared analytical products and evaluations, and provide training. This division of labour positions national centres to address big strategic questions and align with the national policy agenda, while local ICB systems focus on operational and clinical analytics.
