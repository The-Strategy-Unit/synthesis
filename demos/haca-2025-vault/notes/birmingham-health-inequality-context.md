# Birmingham health inequality context

Birmingham is characterised as one of the most ethnically diverse and socioeconomically deprived cities in the United Kingdom. Over 50% of its population belongs to global majority communities, including South Asian, Black African, and Black Caribbean groups. Approximately 88% of Birmingham's wards are more deprived than the England average. This deprivation contributes to significant health disparities; residents in the most deprived areas are three times more likely to develop cardiovascular blood disease compared to those in more affluent areas. The city also faces elevated rates of diabetes and obesity, with 9.3% of adults diagnosed with diabetes and 68% classified as overweight or obese. These statistics underscore the need for targeted preventative healthcare interventions.
