# Legislative and Systemic Barriers in Health Data

Drawing from experience in fintech consultancy, Morley argues that unlocking the benefits of health data requires legislative change rather than just technical solutions. In fintech, post-2008 financial crash regulations empowered users by allowing them to consolidate financial data from different banks into single apps. This required a sequence of legal intervention, data standardization, and user-friendly app design. She contends that similar progress in healthcare is stalled because efforts are currently 'trying at the edges.' True progress requires addressing legislation, data standards, curation, and defining the end-user, rather than treating these issues as decontextualized technical problems.

There is a prevalent myth that the United States has solved many of the healthcare challenges faced by the UK, particularly regarding technology and efficiency. This perception is considered incorrect; the US context is vastly different due to its private healthcare structure, and it has not necessarily achieved better technological outcomes. The reality of the US system is often only apparent to those who have worked on the other side, challenging the assumption that US healthcare is a model of solved problems. This highlights the need for holistic approaches involving legislative change and user-centric design, rather than isolated technical interventions.
