# Building trust through community gatekeepers

To build trust and engage communities in health research and services, it is crucial to utilise trusted gatekeepers rather than relying solely on institutional communications from bodies like the NHS or local councils. For example, Muslim participants indicated they were more likely to listen to religious leaders, such as mosque imams, or local community champions. These figures can convey the importance of health interventions in a culturally resonant way. Engaging community members to speak to their peers helps overcome scepticism and ensures that messages are received as credible and relevant, facilitating better participation in health programmes. This approach supports the development of community-based delivery models, as participants expressed a preference for receiving health checks within their communities rather than at traditional institutional settings like GP surgeries.
