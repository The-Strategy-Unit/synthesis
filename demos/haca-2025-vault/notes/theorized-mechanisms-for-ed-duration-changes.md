# Theorized Mechanisms for ED Duration Changes

Theories regarding changes in ED durations during strikes were categorized into three main areas: changes in demand for ED services, competition for shared hospital resources, and changes in ED operations. Demand theories suggested that patients stayed away or that out-of-hospital services changed. Shared resource theories focused on bed availability affecting boarding times and the accessibility of specialists and diagnostic equipment due to changes in planned care.
<!-- synthesis-claim:e40025cc033d58ebc7da13c61995ba03b5fdae23b6fc51313fc734870f34a5b2 -->

Operational theories included changes in test, treatment, or admission thresholds, and regard for the 4-hour target. Subtler, harder-to-test hypotheses included staff exemption from corporate activities and increased patient compliance due to unusual circumstances. To test these theories, additional data was appended to the existing dataset, including levels of surge provision, ED attendances, community service contracts, GP appointments, hospital surge provision, elective and non-elective bed occupancy, outpatient attendances, diagnostic imaging levels, and admission status.
