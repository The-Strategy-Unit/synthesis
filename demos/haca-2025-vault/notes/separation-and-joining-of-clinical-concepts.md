# Separation and joining of clinical concepts

AI introduces specific separations and unions in clinical practice that fundamentally alter how care is delivered. Separations include: symptoms from diagnosis (allowing symptoms to be rejigged for a diagnosis); knowledge about the body from the body itself (diagnosing data rather than the person); clinical knowledge from the clinician; and care from the clinic. These separations can lead to a disconnect between the patient's lived experience and the algorithmic interpretation of their data.
<!-- synthesis-claim:150f164160265a8af7500ce8203f02d1b562e62265af9614e94a12d0fe60e6bc -->

Unions include: the patient and their data (making the electronic health record the indivisible source of truth); service analytics and clinical decision support (allowing analytics to directly input into electronic health records, impacting worker autonomy); research and practice (moving from siloed testing to real-time deployment); and big tech with healthcare (bringing external data-driven models into the health system). These unions consolidate data and decision-making power, often centralizing control within digital systems rather than distributed clinical teams.
