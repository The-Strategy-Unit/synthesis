# Waste data consolidation and efficiency gains

Prior to the dashboard, waste data was extracted manually from approximately 70 PDFs per week and monthly invoices from over 20 suppliers, requiring tedious copy-and-paste operations into pivot tables. The new system consolidates this data into a consistent structure, saving approximately 27 working days per year. This improvement enhanced compliance monitoring and mandatory reporting to NHS England. The dashboard enabled site-based tracking of waste production and segregation patterns, which was previously impossible. By reviewing weekly and monthly performance, the waste team could target low-performing sites with training, audits, and walkarounds, leading to drastic shifts in segregation patterns and improved operational outcomes.

Obtaining carbon data usage information relies heavily on relationships with suppliers. Suppliers are expected to provide this information, often on invoices, but the quality and format of this data vary. The trust emphasizes the importance of pushing suppliers to provide data in easy-to-use formats. This aspect of the project highlights that technical solutions must be supported by proactive engagement and relationship building with external partners to ensure data availability and consistency. Additionally, the trust integrated AI tools to extract information from PDFs for input into the sustainability dashboard. This process is described as a 'showy' part of the workflow but is noted to be completely reliant on a very consistent format in the source documents. The use of AI is characterized as sparing but effective for ripping information off PDFs, addressing the challenge of manual data entry from inconsistent supplier documents.
