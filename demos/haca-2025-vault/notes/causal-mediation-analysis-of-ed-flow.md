# Causal Mediation Analysis of ED Flow

To understand the drivers behind changes in emergency department durations during strikes, a causal mediation analysis was conducted. The study aimed to distinguish between direct effects, such as staff absence and cover, and indirect effects resulting from other operational changes. The methodology involved generating theories through steering group discussions, descriptive analysis, and staff interviews. Data was appended to test these theories where possible, while untestable theories served as contextual factors.

The analysis used an accelerated failure time model to estimate impacts while controlling for covariants such as age, sex, acuity, chief complaint, time of day, weekend or weekday status, season, and specific ED provider. The objective was to determine if strikes led to changes in a mediator, and if those changes subsequently led to changes in ED duration. This method allowed researchers to attribute specific minutes of duration reduction to particular mechanisms, such as bed occupancy, while controlling for other factors.
