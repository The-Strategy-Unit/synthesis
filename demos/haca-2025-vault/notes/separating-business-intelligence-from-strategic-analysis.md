# Separating business intelligence from strategic analysis

Business intelligence (BI) and strategic analysis are distinct disciplines requiring different management structures, skill sets, and resourcing. BI is data-oriented, aiming to assemble information into accessible formats like tables and charts for decision-makers. It values responsiveness, consistency, and near real-time specificity, operating effectively in clear domains with known cause-and-effect relationships. In contrast, analysis is problem-oriented, addressing strategic, clinical, or operational challenges through insights and recommendations. It draws on methods from statistics, operations research, and epidemiology, valuing perceptiveness and generalizability over immediate specificity.

In many health and care organizations, these functions are lumped together under a single leader. This structure leads to the 'urgent crowding out the important,' where high-volume BI demands displace latent, strategic analytical work. This dynamic is self-perpetuating: analysts often end up performing BI tasks, leading to attrition or deskilling, while teams led by BI experts may lack the capability to develop analytical skills. Consequently, the system becomes 'awash with data' but lacks the insights needed for complex challenges.

To address this, a strategic separation of BI and analysis is proposed. Junior and middle-grade analysts should be managed by experienced analysts rather than generalists. This separation allows for focused development of technical and craft skills, such as problem structuring and communication. Organizations that have separated these functions have seen analysis flourish, enabling analysts to proactively engage with stakeholders and advocate for results rather than waiting for structured problems.
