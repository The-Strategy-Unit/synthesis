# Evolution of data use in the NHS

The integration of data and analysis into the National Health Service (NHS) has evolved significantly over the last three decades. Interest in clinical audit and data-driven decision-making gained momentum in the 1990s, driven by figures such as Don Berwick and Nick Black, who highlighted significant variations in care quality. A pivotal moment occurred with the publication of the 2000 NHS Plan, which was the first government document to explicitly use data to demonstrate that healthcare delivery was suboptimal. This set a changing tone that accelerated the use of analysis in healthcare thinking.

Despite this progress, a substantial gap remains between data availability and its analytical application. While the NHS now possesses vast amounts of national clinical and operational data, it is described as being 'nowhere near' where it needs to be in terms of using this information to inform decision-making. Recent leadership, including the chair of NHS England, advocates for better analysis of existing data to answer outstanding questions, emphasizing that the analytical community remains undervalued despite national emphasis on analytically driven decisions.
