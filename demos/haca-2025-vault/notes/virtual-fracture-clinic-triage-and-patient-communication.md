# Virtual Fracture Clinic Triage and Patient Communication

The reduction in surgery rates associated with the Virtual Fracture Clinic (VFC) was attributed to improved patient triage at the point of presentation in A&E. A set criteria determined which patients met the requirements for the VFC versus those requiring immediate on-call team intervention. Patients with wounds potentially requiring surgery, open wounds, displaced fractures, or complex injuries were directed to the on-call team. Stable fractures and injuries manageable non-operatively were channeled through the VFC, resulting in fewer surgical interventions for the VFC cohort.

To support a diverse, multilingual patient population in a high-deprivation area, specific communication strategies were implemented. These included providing multilingual leaflets available in over 100 languages, introducing QR codes for easy access to information, and offering printed copies for those who needed them. Clear expectations regarding the VFC process were set out to patients. Additionally, posters with quick-reference decision-making pathways and clear guidelines were introduced in the A&E department to assist staff.
