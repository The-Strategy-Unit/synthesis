# Making Data Count Methodology

The Making Data Count program is an NHS initiative led by Sam Riley, a data scientist and improvement leader with nearly 30 years of experience in the NHS. Riley has worked with over 240 NHS boards to strengthen the use of Statistical Process Control (SPC) and is a passionate advocate for using data to drive meaningful change. Launched approximately seven to eight years prior to the presentation, the program has trained around 55,000 people, including analysts and board members. It aims to replace traditional 'rag reporting' (traffic light reporting) with SPC visualizations, helping users distinguish between natural variation and special cause variation to prevent 'sputtling'—overreacting to natural variation—and enabling more appropriate decision-making.

The methodology introduces specific color conventions: orange for concern, blue for improvement, and gray for no change, along with summary icons to simplify complex data. It is integrated into performance dashboards to provide assurance and variation analysis, allowing users to assess metrics against the latest 15 days, 15 weeks, or 15 months. The approach uses SPC charts, including fixed-date national standards and adjustable interval periods (e.g., Western Australia date-adjustable visuals), to identify whether performance changes are due to random noise or specific, actionable events, such as a sustained shift in process or an unusual outlier. This supports the creation of bespoke reports and quick insights on performance and statistical variation.

The program has seen widespread adoption across NHS trusts in England, with many using SPC charts exclusively at board level. It has also expanded internationally, with launches in Norway and adoption in Western Australia (Rottnest Island).
