# Statistical Challenges in Small Control Unit Analysis

Analysing interventions with many small control units presents significant statistical challenges, particularly when attempting to predict outcomes for much larger treated units. This disparity can contribute to wide confidence intervals and reduced precision in impact estimates, even when the methodological framework is robust. Additional complications arise from difficulties in cleanly identifying specific populations, such as homeless individuals, within national datasets and accurately allocating geographic areas to specific services.

Furthermore, synthetic control methods rely on matching trends in the pre-intervention period to implicitly adjust for differences in supporting services across areas. While this approach is agnostic to the specific types of services, it carries a risk if other supporting services change around the same time as the intervention or during the study period, potentially confounding the results. These factors necessitate careful consideration of control unit selection and the potential for heterogeneous trends that may not be fully captured by standard matching procedures.
