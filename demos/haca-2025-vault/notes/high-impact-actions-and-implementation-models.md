# High-Impact Actions and Implementation Models

Four key actions have been identified through structured interviews and testing with 300 partners to enable effective neighbourhood health change. First, using shared data and local intelligence to identify adults with long-term conditions at high or rising risk of escalation. Second, coaching practitioners to activate individuals and support co-management of conditions, leveraging the resourcefulness of families and carers. Third, coordinating care around individuals and neighbourhoods through named multi-agency teams providing 24/7 proactive and reactive care, while rationalising overlapping functions. Fourth, building deep community relationships, particularly with deprived households, using trusted community health and well-being workers to bridge gaps in trust and facilitate early diagnosis and intervention.

These actions are supported by the 'tight-loose-tight' implementation model, which is 'tight' on shared mission, shared accountability, and shared outcomes (specifically whole-person outcomes), while being 'loose' on implementation details to allow for local adaptation. This approach recognises that there is no one-size-fits-all solution and that interventions must be adapted to local contexts. The model aims to create an enabling environment for change by connecting local teams with national policy teams to identify and remove blockers, while co-producing solutions for systemic changes such as funding flows and contracts.
