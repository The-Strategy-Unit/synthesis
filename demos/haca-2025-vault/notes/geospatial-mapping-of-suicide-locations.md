# Geospatial mapping of suicide locations

This method maps suicide locations by combining place of residence data from death registers with location data from 999 emergency calls and hospital records. The approach identifies instances where a suicide occurred outside the individual's place of residence, enabling the identification of specific geographic areas that may require additional safeguarding or intervention. For example, clustering of suicides by drowning near certain bodies of water could support arguments for enhanced safety measures in those locations.

This spatial analysis complements demographic and temporal data to provide a comprehensive view of risk factors. By pinpointing specific geographic hotspots, public health officials can target resources more effectively. The integration of location data with health system interactions allows for a more nuanced understanding of the environmental and contextual factors contributing to suicide risk.
