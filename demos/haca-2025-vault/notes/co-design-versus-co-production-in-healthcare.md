# Co-design versus Co-production in Healthcare

In the context of the Dorset project, co-design referred to the technical process of designing data specifications and ensuring the right data was collected from various organizations. Co-production, on the other hand, involved delivering interventions with the help of primary care partners, local organizations, and the voluntary and community sector. This distinction highlighted the difference between designing the data infrastructure and actively engaging with communities to implement solutions. The project emphasized the importance of involving those with direct experience, such as patients, carers, and residents, through partners who routinely interact with them, rather than relying solely on project leads.

DICE employs a structured process to turn data into actionable intelligence, beginning with co-design. This initial step involves working closely with partners, including clinicians, operational managers, and representatives from the voluntary and community sectors, to build a strong data foundation. Following co-design, the team moves to technical building, creating the data infrastructure required to host reporting products. Throughout this process, DICE utilizes clear trust and governance procedures, treating information governance as an enabler rather than a blocker.
