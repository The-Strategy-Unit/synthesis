# Integrated Care Board strategic commissioning

The Integrated Care Board (ICB) model blueprint recommends a radical simplification of ICB roles, focusing solely on strategic commissioning and removing many performance management functions to regions and providers. This structure creates an opportunity to separate analysis from business intelligence, with providers and regions managing BI functions while ICB analysts focus on strategic commissioning.

However, the strategic commissioning guidance is described as thorough but risks overwhelming ICBs with over 80 expectations. There is concern that ICB analysis may become trapped in ticking off requirements rather than focusing on the endgame: redistributing resources to improve population health outcomes. Past commissioning efforts are described as having failed not by doing too much work, but by failing to take key actions to redistribute resources. A balance must be found between process compliance and actionable commissioning decisions.

There is skepticism about whether ICBs can fully engage in a population health approach given the inevitable distraction of day-to-day operational pressures, such as waiting lists and A&E four-hour targets. These pressures tend to pull focus toward a 'more of the same' BI approach. While guidance frames ICBs as being freed from tactical delivery management, there is uncertainty whether this will hold in practice, especially during political or seasonal crises. The preference is for providers and regions to handle tactical management while ICBs focus on strategic decision-making.
