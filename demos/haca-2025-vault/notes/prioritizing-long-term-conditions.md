# Prioritizing long-term conditions

Prioritization frameworks for health planning can combine multiple metrics to identify focus areas for the next one to five years. Key metrics include prevalence (the size of the problem), unplanned bed day rates (impact on service and population), and inequality scores. In North West London, combining these measures identified diabetes, hypertension, obesity, COPD, and heart failure as priority long-term conditions. While conditions like hypertension have lower unplanned bed day rates, they have high prevalence, whereas conditions like heart failure and dementia have higher unplanned bed day rates. This multi-metric approach creates a key framework for prioritizing specific areas, though it is noted that looking at specific conditions is only part of the problem, and population segments may require different priorities.
