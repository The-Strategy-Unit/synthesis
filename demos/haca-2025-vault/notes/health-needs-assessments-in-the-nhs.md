# Health needs assessments in the NHS

A health needs assessment is a systematic report designed to describe the needs of a population within the National Health Service (NHS). Its primary objectives are to identify where needs exist to enable effective resource allocation, highlight health inequalities across different outcomes, and drive health equity by targeting interventions. By focusing on the population rather than specific services, these assessments aim to emphasize proactive and preventive approaches.

In North West London, such assessments utilize data science techniques to make data accessible to a wider audience, thereby increasing the number of data-driven planning decisions. The process relies on integrated care data warehouses that link diverse datasets, including primary care, secondary care, mental health, community services, and adult social care. Additional datasets, such as EPC certificates and Unique Property Reference Numbers (UPRN), are also integrated. This linkage allows for analysis based on population characteristics rather than service utilization alone and improves the quality of demographic data, such as deprivation, ethnicity, age, and sex, by linking back to patient GP records. This enables the correlation of long-term conditions from primary care with secondary care outcomes, such as unplanned bed day rates.
