# Continuous flow model in hospital operations

The continuous flow model is an operational strategy for hospital admissions that shifts from a 'pull' mentality to a 'push' mentality. In the traditional pull model, wards indicate when they are ready to accept patients. In the push model, wards are notified at specific scheduled times that they will definitely receive admissions, requiring them to be prepared. The goal is to alleviate pressure at the 'front door' (emergency department) and reduce delay-related harm downstream.

This model previously fell out of favor due to challenges in maintaining operational discipline during quieter periods. While discipline is evident during serious incidents, managers and staff tend to relax scheduling intensity when things calm down, leading to a resurgence of delays. This human tendency to ease off strict protocols during low-pressure times undermines the model's effectiveness. Additionally, literature reports on other systems implementing similar models have been mixed, contributing to uncertainty about its efficacy.

Informed by recent modeling work that quantifies the harm of boarding, discussions with hospital managers have led to a decision to reinstate the continuous flow model. The effectiveness of the reinstated model is currently being evaluated, with plans to submit findings as an abstract in the future. The research aims to provide a framework for measuring intervention effectiveness and has been submitted for publication.
