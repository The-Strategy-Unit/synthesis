# Trends in potentially mitigable hospital bed days

National data reveals diverging trends in hospital activity for potentially mitigable categories over the last five years. Hospital activity for end of life care has shown a reduction, with admissions decreasing by 23%, suggesting a shift towards caring for patients at home or in hospices. In contrast, several other categories have seen significant increases in bed days. Chronic ambulatory care sensitive conditions saw a 19% increase in bed days, while acute alcohol-related bed days increased by 35%. Admissions of frail older people with high frailty risk saw a 10% increase in bed days.

For frail older people, national data for 2023–24 indicates that while admissions increased by only about 1%, bed days rose more significantly. This divergence is driven by shifts in length of stay: the proportion of stays under one week is falling, while stays lasting one to two weeks and two weeks or more are increasing. The national average length of stay for this group increased by approximately 8%. These trends highlight that for many categories of potentially avoidable activity, hospital care usage is increasing rather than decreasing, contrasting with the positive shifts seen in end-of-life care.
