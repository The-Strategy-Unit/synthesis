# Framework for quantifying potentially mitigable hospital activity

A strategy unit framework quantifies categories of hospital activity that are potentially mitigable to support healthcare planning. The approach aims to shift care from acute hospitals to community settings by assuming that subsets of hospital activity can be avoided through greater use of existing or new community services. Because directly measuring 'avoided' activity is impossible, measuring rates of potentially mitigable activity provides an indication of whether activity is being avoided or moved. The analysis informs prioritization by identifying high-volume areas, establishing baselines for trend tracking, and enabling comparative analysis to identify learning opportunities from better-performing systems.

The framework defines 92 categories of potentially mitigable activity for the new hospitals program demand and capacity model. A subset of 29 categories was analyzed in detail, focusing on inpatient activity and the potential to move this activity using community services. These categories are not mutually exclusive; for example, an admission could simultaneously fall into ambulatory care sensitive, smoking, and acute respiratory categories. Consequently, summing activity across categories results in double counting. Furthermore, not all activity within these broad categories can or should be avoided; rather, a proportion of the activity, such as 10%, within each category is considered potentially reducible. There is a recognized need for further work to define subsets of these categories more clearly, as broad definitions risk obscuring the specific proportions of activity that could actually be reduced.
