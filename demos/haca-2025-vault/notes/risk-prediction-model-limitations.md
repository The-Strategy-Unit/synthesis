# Risk prediction model limitations

Risk prediction models, or population health management tools, are the most common algorithms deployed in the UK's health and care system, used for risk stratification such as predicting avoidable emergency admissions. Evidence suggests that once deployed in the real world, some of these models are no more accurate than a coin toss. They often fail to make a difference because they flag individuals already known to be high risk by clinicians, or they capture people not actually at risk, driving up unnecessary care.

Crucially, they do not work equally well for everyone; individuals not represented in the original training dataset are largely ignored, leading to worse care for those groups. The gap between statistical accuracy in constrained environments and real-world complexity is significant. Bias in AI extends beyond historical data representation to include how information is evaluated and how care pathways differ from model expectations. A significant concern is the emergence of new biases where algorithms infer sensitive attributes, such as race or ethnicity, from clinical data like chest X-rays, replicating historical disparities in treatment.
