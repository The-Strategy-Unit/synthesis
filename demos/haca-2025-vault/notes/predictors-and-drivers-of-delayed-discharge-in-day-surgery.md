# Predictors and drivers of delayed discharge in day surgery

Delayed discharge in day surgery units is operationally defined as any patient not discharged on the same day as their procedure. While the target performance metric is set at 95% same-day discharges, initial data indicated that approximately 30% of patients experienced delays. A logistic regression analysis of 6,000 patients identified that patient health conditions, including frailty, age, sex, and socioeconomic deprivation, were not statistically significant predictors of discharge delays. This finding suggests homogeneous quality of care across clinicians and no evidence of health inequalities in access to pain relief or discharge probability based on deprivation status.
<!-- synthesis-claim:49835e4e2411cb2fb51194d7423c7ead6138308501c13fcaadf2018636dd9a39 -->

Instead, significant predictors included service-related factors, medical practice variables, and temporal elements. The type of procedure, specialty, and anesthesia type were key determinants. Notably, patients operated on Saturdays were twice as likely to be discharged on time compared to weekday patients, attributed to a staffing model where staff leave immediately upon task completion, preventing the expansion of tasks to fill shift time. Conversely, Sunday operations showed a five times higher likelihood of delay, reflecting emergency cases with reduced staffing rather than standard elective procedures.
<!-- synthesis-claim:49835e4e2411cb2fb51194d7423c7ead6138308501c13fcaadf2018636dd9a39 -->

The analysis also highlighted the impact of 'border patients'—individuals placed in day surgery beds due to capacity constraints elsewhere in the hospital. These patients can account for up to a third of the unit's population, posing a risk to efficiency gains derived from day surgery-specific optimizations. Safeguarding these gains requires preventing the dilution of the unit's purpose by inappropriate patient placement.
