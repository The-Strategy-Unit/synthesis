# Lessons from Past Healthcare Change Initiatives

Previous large-scale change initiatives aiming for a 'left shift' in care have often failed due to several key factors. Success was frequently viewed narrowly through the lens of the NHS, focusing on hospital activity rather than whole-person outcomes. Change was treated as technical and linear rather than complex and adaptive, ignoring the necessity of trusted relationships. Additionally, national regulation, performance frameworks, and funding flows did not keep pace with local operational changes, meaning improvements were not 'hardwired' and could reverse with leadership changes. Finally, a lack of integrated data sets hindered the ability to understand the costs of shifting left and measure effective interventions.

Evaluating the impact of health interventions involves complex analytical work, including identifying appropriate counterfactuals to understand what would have happened without the intervention. The healthcare system is dynamic, with new initiatives constantly being embedded, making static analysis difficult. There is also a 'substitution effect' where reducing admissions in one area might lead to increased demand elsewhere, or external factors like a severe flu virus strain can skew data. Understanding which specific interventions have the biggest impact requires linking whole-person outcomes with whole-person interventions, considering both the 'what' and the 'how' of delivery.
