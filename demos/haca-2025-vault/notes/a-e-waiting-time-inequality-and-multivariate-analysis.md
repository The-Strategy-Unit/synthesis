# A&E waiting time inequality and multivariate analysis

An investigation into Accident and Emergency (A&E) waiting times was prompted by a 2024 Nuffield Trust report identifying longer waits for Black, Asian, and mixed ethnicity children. To address the limited multivariate analysis in that report, population health intelligence specialists at Royal Wolverhampton NHS Trust conducted a deeper analysis using data from Walsall and Wolverhampton hospital trusts between April 2022 and March 2024. The study aimed to determine whether observed ethnic disparities persisted after controlling for clinical and demographic confounders.

Initial exploratory analysis confirmed the Nuffield Trust’s findings: waiting times were left-skewed, with a mean of 256 minutes compared to a median of 173 minutes. Longer waits were associated with younger age groups, specific admission statuses, and higher patient acuity. Variations were also noted between the two hospital trusts despite their similar geographic coverage. These initial findings prompted a rigorous multivariate investigation to isolate the impact of ethnicity and deprivation from other factors.

The subsequent modelling revealed that while ethnicity and deprivation indices appeared significant in unadjusted analyses, they did not have a significant impact on waiting times once variables such as age, admission status, and acuity were accounted for. This suggests that the observed inequalities may be driven by clinical complexity and demographic factors rather than systemic bias in waiting times alone. The findings shifted the focus of equity concerns towards other areas, such as patients leaving before being seen.
