# Care Data Spaces project overview

Care Data Spaces is a collaborative, co-designed research project currently in its early stages, led by researchers from the University of Birmingham and the ESRC Centre for Care in partnership with West Midlands ADASS. Funded by the ESRC, the project aims to establish a sustainable data system that enables stakeholders to understand and improve social care support and well-being outcomes. Key colleagues involved include Pete Jackson, Paul Johnson, and Caroline Volles Loos.

The project seeks to integrate operational research and learning into business-as-usual models, using routine data to inform practice and decision-making. A central tenet is co-design and co-production, involving a community of practice, a lived experience panel, and practitioners working daily in the field. This approach aims to empower these groups to set the agenda, research questions, and priorities, ensuring outputs meet sector needs and remain sustainable post-project. The focus is on the lives people want to lead, emphasizing choice and control in social care experiences.

By working with people with lived experience, commissioners, practitioners, and policymakers, the project seeks to develop digital learning tools that inform daily practice and bridge academic and practice boundaries. It commits to the open science framework, making files, code, and analyses publicly available to support capacity building and ensure analytical work can be sustained and replicated by practitioners after the project concludes.
