# Deprivation and healthcare use inequalities

Deprivation is strongly associated with higher healthcare use across all time periods surrounding elective care, with the most pronounced differences observed in emergency care. The relative difference in emergency care use between the most and least deprived quintiles was greatest during the waiting period. This suggests that deprived populations face particular challenges while on waiting lists, potentially leading to increased reliance on emergency services due to unmet needs or lack of alternative support.

These inequalities were observed across most points of healthcare delivery and align with broader literature on health disparities. In response to evidence linking socioeconomic status to healthcare outcomes and waiting list management, practical applications have emerged. For example, in Grampian, deprivation data has been added to the information roster supplied to clinicians to aid in prioritisation decisions. This represents an effort to mitigate the impact of deprivation on waiting times and outcomes by explicitly considering socioeconomic factors in clinical prioritisation.
