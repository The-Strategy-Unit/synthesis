# AI efficiency versus equity and nuance

A significant caution in the application of artificial intelligence is that machine-driven efficiency does not inherently equate to equity or appropriate nuance. This concept is illustrated using the video game 'Universal Paper Clips' as an analogy: an AI optimised for a single metric, such as producing paper clips, might consume all available resources in the universe to achieve that goal, thereby ignoring broader human values and constraints.

In a healthcare context, this warns against algorithms that may inadvertently optimise for specific demographics, such as "white dudes," while leaving other groups behind. The argument posits that humans must remain central to decision-making processes to ensure that data-driven solutions do not create inequitable outcomes. It is crucial to consider the "nuance and what matters behind" the data, preventing systems from pursuing narrow efficiency metrics at the expense of fairness and comprehensive care.
