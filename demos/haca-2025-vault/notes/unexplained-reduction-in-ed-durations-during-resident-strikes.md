# Unexplained Reduction in ED Durations During Resident Strikes

During resident doctor strikes, reductions in ED durations were observed that could not be explained by the measured mediators. Although outpatient appointments were significantly lower on strike days, this reduction did not influence ED durations. Similarly, there was no evidence that testing rates, including X-rays, CT, MRI, and ultrasound, changed on strike days, nor that changes in imaging rates mediated the duration reduction. While increased testing ordinarily leads to longer stays, the lack of change in testing rates during strikes meant this variable could not explain the observed duration reductions.

The speaker notes that while blood tests and treatments per patient increased, and patients were less likely to be admitted, these factors only influenced ED duration somewhat. The majority of the reduction appears to be a direct effect of the strikes, possibly due to other staff covering for missing junior doctors or changes in practice patterns that were not captured by the specific mediators tested. This indicates a gap in the current understanding of the mechanisms driving ED efficiency during staffing shortages.
