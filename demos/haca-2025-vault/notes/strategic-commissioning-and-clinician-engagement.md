# Strategic commissioning and clinician engagement

Strategic commissioning is described as a notion invented within the NHS with limited published evidence regarding its success. It presents a difficult agenda for ICBs, which are tasked with rethinking their fundamental purpose and allocating resources to meet population needs. This task cannot be answered purely technocratically; it requires engaging with citizen choices and preferences. Engaging clinicians in data analysis is considered a productive route to solving healthcare problems, potentially more effective than pursuing abstract policy ideas. Successful collaboration involves working with clinicians who have a clear sense of the problem, access to a network of colleagues for testing, and the ability to contribute to building credible assumptions for models. Analysts are characterized fundamentally as problem solvers who should identify what problems clinicians need solved and determine how their analytical skills can contribute. There is also an emerging opportunity to use AI and agent functionalities to automate aspects of strategic commissioning, such as initial scoping for recommissioning pathways, allowing analysts to receive preliminary analysis without performing all the manual legwork.
