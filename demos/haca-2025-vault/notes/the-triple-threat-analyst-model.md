# The triple threat analyst model

The 'triple threat analyst' framework expands the role of public health and care intelligence analysts beyond technical execution to include knowledge transfer and stakeholder engagement. The model posits that a well-rounded professional operates across three interconnected components. First, **Analysis** involves developing modern analytical techniques, moving beyond traditional tools like Excel or SPSS to coding methods such as R and Python. This allows for greater customization in descriptive, exploratory, explanatory, forecasting, and impact estimation work. Second, **Knowledge to Action (K2A)** focuses on ensuring findings are shared, understood, and acted upon. This requires involvement in conversations throughout the project lifecycle to set expectations and ensure stakeholders value the results. Third, **Teaching and Knowledge Transfer** entails sharing methodology and best practices to develop an 'intelligent customer' and engage in communities of practice. This includes presenting work, sharing plans, and providing feedback.

The model encourages analysts to step out of their comfort zones to engage in facilitation, leadership, and broader system involvement, rather than remaining isolated in technical work. By embracing challenges and integrating deeply into the organizational system, analysts can achieve a more fulfilling and effective professional experience. Some variations of the concept refer to a 'quadruple threat' model, emphasizing networking and communication alongside the core technical and transfer skills.
