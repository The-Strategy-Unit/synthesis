# Whole Person Outcomes and Strength-Based Care

Whole person outcomes extend beyond traditional NHS service utilisation metrics to include wider factors important to individuals, such as social isolation, housing, financial stability, and mental health. This approach involves 'what matters to you' conversations to understand how symptoms play out in the context of a person's family, household, and living environment. The goal is to make higher-quality, personalised care decisions by seeing the whole picture, which supports the transition to value-based care. It requires a mindset shift from treating body parts to caring for whole persons and households, recognising the interdependence of health factors.
<!-- synthesis-claim:1ab79c84705d8722748abea67f7a8bc9e66033b5c3850661b91d70fd081b5656 -->

This is underpinned by a strength-based approach, which shifts from a deficit-based model to one that recognises and builds upon the strengths and resourcefulness of individuals, families, and communities. This approach involves asking 'what matters to you' rather than 'what is the matter with you', and focusing on 'doing with' rather than 'doing to' patients. It aims to dissolve power imbalances and bureaucracy, allowing for more creative and personalised care decisions. The approach recognises that 90% of factors affecting health activation are outside the healthcare system, such as social isolation and financial instability.
