# Brent Adult Social Care Multi-State Transition Model

The Brent Adult Social Care Multi-State Transition Model is a longitudinal study conducted by Brent Council in collaboration with other council colleagues to analyse transitions between adult social care packages. The project aims to inform future care provision planning, estimate care home needs, and identify proactive measures to prevent escalation to intensive care. The study utilises data from the Whole Systems Integrated Care Data Resource, covering over 2.4 million patients in North West London, including linked GP, hospital, community, and social care records. The analysis focuses on de-identified patient-level data for Brent adult social care clients between January 2020 and December 2024.

The modelling employs the R package MSM, which assumes that transition probabilities depend only on the current state. The study faced challenges with model convergence, requiring the simplification of covariants to broad ethnicity categories and two age groups. The final dataset included approximately 7,000 adults with complete data, observing about 26,800 transitions over five years of follow-up. Brent Council's annual spend on adult social care is approximately £90 million for care at home and £38 million for residential care. New requests for support have been rising by about 5% each year, with the most rapid growth in demand occurring within the working-age population.
