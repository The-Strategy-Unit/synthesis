# Modern data infrastructure for adult social care

A proposed integrated, transparent, and secure data infrastructure is designed to address fragmented and inconsistent insights in local authorities. It combines public adult social care data, geographical information, and local context to provide customized reports and interactive data sites. Key features include data analysis at scale, integration support for commissioning and workforce planning, and open science practices. The system aims to support quicker decision-making, CQC assurance readiness, and place-based planning while reducing burden on constrained workforces.

The project proposes creating a virtual data space and dashboard for interactive mapping and analytics. This infrastructure will bring together aggregated mandated data, census information, and geographical data into one place to inform decision-making and improve understanding of intersectional inequalities and need. Insights and analyses are intended to be upscaled, automated, and replicated. Initially focusing on priority areas identified by a dozen local authorities in the West Midlands, the system aims to automate these priorities across local authorities and eventually upscale nationally.

A key component involves working closely with 14 local authorities in the West Midlands to analyze client-level records and link them to other administrative records. The goal is to develop a safe, ethical, and reproducible process for the preparation and analysis of client-level data that captures individual care journeys and lived experiences. This work aims to inform routing practice and learning support in adult social care, contributing to the implementation and evaluation of the data system.
