# Community co-creation of knowledge

Treating communities as co-creators of knowledge yields richer, more accurate, and meaningful insights than studying them as passive subjects. This approach leverages lived experience to expose hidden assumptions and cultural realities that shape engagement with health services. By working with communities, researchers and policymakers can design interventions that are more equitable, effective, and human-centred. This shift is critical for achieving health equity, as data alone is insufficient to drive meaningful change; dialogue is essential for translating insights into actionable strategies. Participatory action research is presented as a highly effective model for this purpose, shifting the approach from extractive data collection to genuine partnership. A key challenge identified is that co-designing research materials with community providers is time-intensive, highlighting the need for sufficient lead-in time and early engagement to streamline processes and enhance material quality.
