# Metaphors for data utility in management

Two visual metaphors illustrate the functional differences between business intelligence (BI) and strategic analysis in management contexts. The first depicts a manager lost on a cold, wet hillside at night. BI is likened to a large box of matches that dimly illuminates immediate surroundings, helping with the next step but failing to provide direction or a path to safety. Analysis, conversely, is compared to well-built torches or beacons strategically located to guide the manager through the darkness.

The second metaphor compares BI to a packet of crisps: quickly consumed but ultimately unsatisfying and unsustainable. Analysis is likened to carefully prepared, varied meals delivered periodically, which sustain the manager over the long term. Both metaphors emphasize that while BI offers rapid, superficial data useful for tactical responses, it is inadequate for complex problems. Strategic analysis provides the deep, well-crafted insights necessary for long-term problem resolution and navigation.
