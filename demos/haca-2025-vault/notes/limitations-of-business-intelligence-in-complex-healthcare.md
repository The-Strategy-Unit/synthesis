# Limitations of business intelligence in complex healthcare

Business intelligence (BI) is effective for tactical responses in clear domains where cause-and-effect relationships are known, simple, and fixed. It helps users understand new territories, generate hypotheses, and operate within uncontentious frameworks. However, BI is inadequate for addressing the complex and complicated enduring problems facing the healthcare system, where cause-and-effect relationships are intricate, dynamic, or unknown.

In these complex domains, reliance on BI often crowds out deeper analysis. Managers are left with dashboards, simple counts, rates, summary statistics, time series, benchmarking, confidence intervals, or control charts. These products provide rapid but superficial data that illuminates only immediate surroundings. They fail to offer the strategic insights required for long-term problem resolution, leaving the organization unable to address systemic challenges such as equity or structural funding issues.
