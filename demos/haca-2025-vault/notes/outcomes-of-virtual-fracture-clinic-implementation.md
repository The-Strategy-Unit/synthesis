# Outcomes of Virtual Fracture Clinic Implementation

The implementation of the virtual fracture clinic (VFC) at North Middlesex Hospital resulted in measurable improvements in efficiency, compliance, and cost. Over 5.2 weeks, 484 referrals were processed. 32% of patients were discharged safely with advice, while 68% required escalation to a face-to-face review. Patients seen in the VFC were less likely to require surgery compared to those in the face-to-face clinic, indicating improved triage. Following the VFC introduction, total face-to-face attendances fell by 21%, follow-ups decreased by 29%, new patient visits dropped by 4%, and walk-ins reduced by 8%.

Operational metrics improved significantly. Breach compliance improved with breaches falling from 37.4% to 14.4%, representing a 61% relative reduction. The average wait for a clinical review reduced by 3.5 days to 2 days. DNA rates also improved substantially; for new patients, the rate dropped from 23.5% to 8.3%, and for follow-up appointments, it decreased from 30% to 17.6%. These changes contributed to a more predictable and less congested clinic environment. Projected cost savings from VFC discharges over the 5.2-week period amounted to just over £10,000, extrapolating to just over £100,000 per year based on 252 working days.
