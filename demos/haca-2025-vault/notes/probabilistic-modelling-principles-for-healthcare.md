# Probabilistic Modelling Principles for Healthcare

The Strategy Unit advocates for predictive models in healthcare to be probabilistic rather than providing single point estimates. This stance is based on the argument that point estimates are inherently wrong when facing future uncertainty. Key principles of this approach include transparency, open-source availability, reproducibility, and collaborative modelling. The goal is to ensure that analyses for high-stakes decisions, such as building hospitals, are accessible in the public record and subject to scrutiny.

Building robust simulation models requires a multi-disciplinary approach, involving collaboration across diverse staff groups, including both analytical and non-analytical personnel. This process involves understanding stakeholder assumptions and worldviews, as much of the work around modelling—getting from data to actionable insight—is not obvious. Documenting assumptions openly allows others to understand, reuse, and build upon the modelling components. However, true transparency and reproducibility can be hindered by significant technical dependencies, such as the need for specific infrastructure like HARRIS (a hospital information system), making it non-trivial for external parties to replicate the models despite the availability of open-source code.
