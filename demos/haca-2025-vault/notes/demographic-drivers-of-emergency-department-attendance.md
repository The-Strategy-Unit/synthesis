# Demographic drivers of emergency department attendance

Analysis of emergency department data moves beyond isolated protected characteristics to examine how factors such as ethnicity, deprivation, and age intersect with patient flow. In adult attendances, demand is concentrated in specific age and ethnicity brackets rather than deprivation brackets. The primary hotspot of activity is among white patients aged 50 to 69, with demand skewing further towards older patients within this group. While deprived populations present with more frequency and less acuity, disaggregated data shows that ethnicity and age are stronger drivers of attendance volume in this cohort than deprivation alone.

In pediatric attendances, demand is higher among younger patient groups. Similar to adults, white ethnicity patients are dominant in attendance numbers. However, unlike the adult cohort where deprivation plays a significant role in frequency, age is the primary driver of demand in pediatrics. There is significant demographic intersectionality, where white patients tend to be older, suggesting that observed ethnicity effects on outcomes may actually be age effects. It is noted that while Leicester City is plural, the wider county has a white majority population; therefore, the data may not be fully representative of the city's specific demographic composition.
