# AI capabilities, misconceptions, and future potential

AI systems, including large language models, operate on prediction rather than genuine understanding. They predict the next most likely word or outcome based on historical data, functioning more like a 'stochastic parrot' that repeats patterns without comprehension. This leads to errors such as misinterpreting document structures or providing inconsistent answers. There is a significant conflation between general AI and generative AI, driven by the popularity of tools like ChatGPT. This causes problems because generative AI is not suitable for all tasks, such as predictive modeling, yet it dominates the conversation, reinforcing a 'magic' perception of AI.

While high-profile diagnostic AI often struggles with scalability, simpler, operational uses of AI can deliver significant impact. For example, using Microsoft Copilot to redesign school vaccination booking processes improved uptake and consistency. These 'boring' applications address immediate workflow inefficiencies. Greater excitement lies in AI's potential to overcome human cognitive limits in system-level tasks, such as understanding 'causes of causes,' modeling complex system dynamics, and predicting staffing needs. However, achieving novel insights remains a difficult, unsolved challenge due to the vast, unconstrained complexity of healthcare compared to constrained game environments.
