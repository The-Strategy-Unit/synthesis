# Sectoral distribution of UK end-of-life spending

Public expenditure on end-of-life care in the UK is distributed across three main sectors: healthcare, social care, and social security. Healthcare accounts for over half of the total spend, estimated at £11.7 billion. Within healthcare, hospital care is the primary driver, with unplanned hospital care contributing more than half of total healthcare spend, followed by planned hospital care. Hospice care represents a small proportion, at 1.7% for outpatient community care and 1.8% for inpatient care.

Social care constitutes 22% of the total, with almost £5 billion spent. Local authority-funded residential and nursing care are the largest components within this sector. Social security payments account for a quarter of the expenditure. For those of pensionable age, the state pension contributes the majority of this spend, while working-age individuals may receive personal independence payments, attendance allowance, universal credit, carers allowance, and housing benefit.
