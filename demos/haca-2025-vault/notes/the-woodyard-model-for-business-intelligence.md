# The Woodyard Model for Business Intelligence

The Woodyard Model is a business intelligence operating framework and analytical commercial product cycle approach designed to reduce waste, improve workflow, and enhance collaboration. Introduced by Marc Farr, the model employs the metaphor of a sawmill: raw data ('logs') are processed in a specific, sequential order rather than being hacked at simultaneously by multiple teams. This approach aims to establish a shared pathway for data work, ensuring that efforts are coordinated, reusable, and grounded in craft rather than just tools.

The process involves distinct stages: data development (understanding raw inputs), data engineering (milling data into clean, repeatable structures or 'planks'), modeling and definitions (agreeing on standard definitions for components), data visualization (designing usable outputs or 'furniture'), and analytics and planning (determining how insights are applied). A key feature of the model is its use as a formal mechanism for accepting or rejecting work requests, thereby preventing fragmented efforts and ensuring that logic resides in a single underlying model capable of feeding multiple products.

Implementation of the Woodyard Model emphasizes explicit roles, upfront data mapping, and a focus on psychological safety and neurodiversity awareness within analyst teams. It prioritizes collaboration over downstreaming work, ensuring that different teams do not simply pass tasks along sequentially. Key practices include weekly check-ins, active group chats, and rapid iterations. The focus is on chasing the 'right insight' and 'golden truth' rather than perfection, aiming to reduce friction between users and analytical decision-making. Clear role definition helps avoid confusion and prevent bottlenecks.

The model has been applied in the development of the GP Resilience Matrix, where a 'woodyard approach'—characterized by co-working rather than simply passing work downstream—has proven effective. In this context, clear roles are defined for data visualization teams, population health management teams, clinical leads, product owners, and technical owners. Protected time with business owners ensures that the tool remains grounded in the reality of ground-level operations, such as GP practice workflows. By balancing speed with quality and surfacing risks early, the model seeks to create a culture where team members can contribute their best thinking without fear.
