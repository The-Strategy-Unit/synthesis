# Evaluation Outcomes of Dorset Project

The evaluation of the Dorset project, conducted around May, showed a small reduction in the average number of primary care visits for the cohort of people involved. However, the project did not achieve all four of its initial outcomes. The evaluation acknowledged that a one-year period was insufficient to observe significant system-level changes, but the connections and meetings established were considered pivotal. The project continued to monitor and plan for further evaluations in the coming years to assess long-term impacts. The success of the project was attributed to the collaborative efforts and the ability to make early interventions, preventing crises and reducing the burden on health services.

The evaluation noted that while the project did not claim to catch every person at risk, using data helped uncover potential gaps and improve practices over time. The collaboration with Dorset Police Service added a layer of protection against fraudulent activity, supporting those at risk of financial exploitation. The project emphasized the importance of involving those with direct experience, such as patients, carers, and residents, through partners who routinely interact with them, rather than relying solely on project leads.
