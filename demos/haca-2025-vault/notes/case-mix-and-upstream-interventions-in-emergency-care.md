# Case mix and upstream interventions in emergency care

Decomposition exercises reveal that for admitted patients, a significant portion of both ethnicity and deprivation gaps in waiting times is explained by case mix factors such as age, acuity, frailty, and arrival time—factors determined before patients arrive at the emergency department. Clinical information indicates that white populations generally present with higher acuity, measured using early warning scores and Dynamic Priority Scores (DPS). This higher acuity, combined with their older age profile, contributes to the case mix differences. Consequently, admitted pathways, despite showing the greatest gaps, offer the lowest potential for change through secondary care operational tweaks.

Conversely, for non-admitted patients, particularly regarding deprivation gaps, there is a larger proportion of unexplained variance, indicating greater potential for ED operational changes to influence outcomes. To address disparities for admitted patients, interventions must target community-level factors upstream of ED arrival. Analysis suggests that to maximize impact with limited resources, interventions should focus upstream of patient presentation, outside the ED. This approach helps distinguish between medically driven demand, which is more prevalent in admitted pathways, and non-admitted pathways, informing how integrated neighborhood teams deploy medical versus social models of care.
