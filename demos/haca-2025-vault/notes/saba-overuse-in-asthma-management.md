# SABA overuse in asthma management

Over-reliance on short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABAs), such as salbutamol, is a critical metric in asthma management. Clinical guidelines aim to limit SABA use to fewer than six inhalers per year, encouraging the use of preventative inhaled steroids instead. High SABA use (six or more per year) is associated with poor asthma control and increased mortality. Data from Dorset indicates that while 30% of patients do not use SABAs at all, a small percentage (2.5%) use 12 or more, indicating terrible asthma control. Reducing SABA overuse is linked to improved health outcomes and reduced respiratory admissions. The desired clinical distribution involves reducing SABA usage and increasing the use of inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), shifting from 'blue inhalers' to modern treatments to prevent exacerbations requiring oral steroid tablets like prednisolone.
