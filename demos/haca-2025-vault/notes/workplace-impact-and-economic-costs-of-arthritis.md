# Workplace impact and economic costs of arthritis

Arthritis and musculoskeletal (MSK) conditions significantly limit employment capabilities, affecting not only work performance but also the ability to work at all. Half of surveyed individuals reported that arthritis impacted their ability to work in some way, with nearly four in 10 of those having to stop working entirely. In the UK, arthritis and MSK conditions are among the leading causes of workplace absence, accounting for 23.4 million lost work days in 2022, an increase from 23.3 million in 2021. For those unable to continue working, over a third of retirees indicated that arthritis played a part in their decision to retire. Specific impacts include the inability to perform physical job duties or meet the demands of management roles, such as sitting through long meetings or working extended hours.

Living with arthritis incurs extra and hidden costs beyond direct medical expenses. These include necessary home adaptations, specialized equipment, and additional travel needs for accessing treatment. Four in 10 people reported increased personal expenditures in the past 12 months due to their condition. When viewed through a health inequalities lens, both decreased earning ability and increased personal expenditure disproportionately affect individuals from lower social grades and those with autoimmune inflammatory conditions. These financial burdens contribute to the risk of poverty and hardship for those managing the condition.
