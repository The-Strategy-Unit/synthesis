# Catalysts and mental models for NHS analytics

Several initiatives have contributed to the advancement of analysis within the NHS, shifting it from a 'minority sport' to a more recognized discipline. Key catalysts include the Health Foundation's Advancing Applied Analytics grant program, which provided small grants to organizations to give analysts space and time to apply good methods to organizational problems. Other significant factors include the emergence of NHSR and Python communities, the Goldacre Review, and events such as HACA. Despite these advances, analysis has not yet fully taken off or maximized its potential impact.

A useful mental model for healthcare analysis involves finding correspondence between three distinct domains: the problems facing the health and care system, the available data, and the analytical methods. The goal is to identify a 'eureka moment' where a specific method can be applied to an available dataset to address or shed insight on a specific problem. This approach frames analysis as a bridge between data availability and strategic or operational challenges.

There is also confusion regarding the definitions of data science, business intelligence, and analysis. Data science is described as having introduced machine learning and reproducible methods that improved traditional analytical thinking. However, data scientists are often treated as 'jacks of all trades,' expected to handle everything from database building to forecasting, which dilutes deep expertise. It is argued that data scientists should be allowed to focus on their specific strengths and work closely with analysts, rather than being thrown at every problem.
