# Collective Learning and Psychological Safety

A learning strategy where all team members attend sessions simultaneously, typically every two weeks. This approach ensures colleagues learn new techniques at the same time, facilitating the formation of learning groups for implementation support. A key benefit is simplified quality assurance, as peers possess the same newly acquired technical knowledge, allowing for easier verification and collaboration.

This model fosters psychological safety, a cultural condition that allows analysts to admit gaps in knowledge and share beginner-level understanding without fear of judgment. Peer-led facilitation, based on the principle that 'teaching is learning,' normalizes beginner status and encourages collaboration. Every team member is expected to facilitate learning sessions, enhancing the sustainability of the program by preventing reliance on a few individuals. This culture of learning together has been cited as a factor in attracting and retaining talent by demonstrating clear opportunities for professional growth.
