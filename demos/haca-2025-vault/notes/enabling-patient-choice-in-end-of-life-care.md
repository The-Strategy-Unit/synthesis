# Enabling patient choice in end-of-life care

Realising patient choice at the end of life requires navigating a complex landscape of clinical, logistical, and systemic factors. While there is a strong emphasis on restoring agency through conversations about what matters to the patient, achieving preferred outcomes—such as dying at home—is frequently hindered by barriers. These include difficulties in initiating advanced care planning, shortages of essential equipment and staff, and geographic inequities in service provision. Furthermore, the increasing complexity of conditions managed in the community, alongside a lack of standardised national data, complicates the ability to consistently meet patient preferences.
