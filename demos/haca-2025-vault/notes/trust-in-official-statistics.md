# Trust in Official Statistics

Trust is described as the fundamental currency of official statistics. Building this trust requires honesty about confidence levels and uncertainty in analysis, communicated in an approachable manner. The public's understanding of complex concepts, such as R numbers during the pandemic, demonstrated the importance of clear and repeated explanation by experts. To maintain trust, statisticians must ensure that data collection measures what truly matters, even if it requires pausing or discontinuing certain time series.
<!-- synthesis-claim:2dc66f67c8245a60dea26bb599bc905749cfcdfe51bd4a15b2f81a66560350b6 -->

Analysts should explain confidence and uncertainty in plain language that decision-makers can understand, rather than relying on technical metrics like P-values. The focus should be on clarifying what the analysis can and cannot tell the audience. Cutting through difficult concepts with clear insights helps build trust and ensures that the limitations and strengths of the data are accurately represented. Leadership programs are implemented to give statistical managers the confidence to make bold, pragmatic changes that prioritize quality and relevance over routine production, thereby holding truth to power against potentially misleading conclusions.
