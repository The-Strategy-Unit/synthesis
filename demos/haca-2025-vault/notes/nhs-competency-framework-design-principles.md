# NHS Competency Framework Design Principles

The NHS Competency Framework (NCF) is designed as a modular, adaptable tool described metaphorically as 'build-a-bear', 'pick-a-mix', or 'Lego build'. It aims to provide consistent language for roles while allowing organisations to construct job descriptions relevant to specific needs. The framework prioritises immediate usability for the current NHS landscape over idealised structural separations. It is intended to support benchmarking against a national peer-reviewed standard and to facilitate training and career mapping through tools like Analyst X.

The development of the NCF follows a transparent, collaborative methodology. The process begins with literature searching to identify and incorporate existing content, avoiding duplication, with support from organisations like the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA). Initial drafts are created and then subjected to iterative review through workshops. Depending on the complexity of the topic, two to five workshops are held, involving representation from across the system to refine the content and ensure broad engagement. Following workshops, the framework undergoes user testing with the wider community to gather feedback on proposed changes. Final feedback is incorporated into change logs detailing accepted changes and reasoning. Updates are scheduled for publication, with more complex changes potentially deferred to future versions.
