# National Diabetes Learning Health System

The National Diabetes Learning Health System is a framework designed to integrate clinical practice with research within the National Diabetes Audit in England and Wales. Its primary objective is to improve care for the 3.8 million people living with diabetes by reducing life-altering and life-shortening complications. The system achieves this by embedding randomized trials directly into routine audit processes, allowing for the evaluation of quality improvement interventions against standard care or delayed facilitation.

This approach was inspired by strategies from Langone Health in New York, which demonstrated how targeted interventions—such as modifying prescription reminders or discontinuing ineffective mailed reminders—could improve patient outcomes and reduce costs. Similarly, the Behavioral Insights Unit’s use of randomization to optimize messaging for organ donation registration provided a model for testing communication strategies within routine administrative workflows. These examples underscore the value of using randomization in everyday operations to identify the most effective approaches for public health and clinical initiatives.
<!-- synthesis-claim:20754c8b3bdb7cd6f8e02cabbee0d273c698420c13b1dc765468f7481736bea5 -->

The ethical foundation for these embedded trials rests on the principle of equipoise. Despite the long-standing provision of quality improvement collaboratives, there remains genuine uncertainty regarding their superiority over standard care. This uncertainty justifies the use of randomized comparisons to determine whether such interventions yield incremental improvements in patient care, ensuring that resources are directed toward strategies that demonstrably enhance outcomes.
