# Financial analysis of lower limb surgery at Royal Free Hospital

A study conducted at the Royal Free Hospital NHS Foundation Trust examined the financial performance of lower limb surgery at a main hospital site. Data collected over a six-week period covered 13 surgical lists involving hips, knees, and foot and ankle procedures. The analysis revealed a substantial net loss of nearly £100,000, with total income of £266,000 against costs of £366,000.

Knee procedures, comprising 28 cases, incurred a loss of over £51,000, averaging approximately £1,800 per case. Hip procedures were the most financially damaging, with seven cases resulting in a total loss of £40,000 and a steep per-case loss of roughly £5,700. Foot and ankle surgeries also operated at a loss, averaging £855 per case, though they demonstrated greater efficiency than hip or knee pathways. The findings highlight a systemic financial disparity where main sites, which manage complex cases and complications, suffer from underpayment relative to actual costs.
